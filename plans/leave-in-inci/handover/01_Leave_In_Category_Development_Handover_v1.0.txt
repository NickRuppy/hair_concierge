3. Category mission
Build an evidence-backed ingredient-research and matching standard for conventional leave-on hair-conditioning products sold in Germany/EU. The standard must classify what an exact formula is likely to do, how it creates the effect, what trade-offs it introduces and which user contexts it is likely to suit—while keeping formula plausibility, finished-product evidence and user fit separate.
Required deliverables
Leave-In Research & Classification Standard v1.0 in DOCX and agent-context Markdown.
Calibration workbook with 10–12 exact German-market products, GTINs, formulas and blind reviewer sheets.
Proposed reference key, agreement dashboard, disagreement log and change log.
Three to five adversarial stress-test products and tightened rules.
Lean application-facing matching profile with property-specific evidence records.
Runtime research prompt for a newly uploaded leave-in product.
Version and re-review rules for formula, GTIN and category-boundary changes.
4. Proposed category charter
4.1 Working definition
A leave-in is a cosmetic hair product intentionally left on the fiber after application, whose primary or co-primary function is conditioning, detangling, smoothing, frizz control, curl support, heat-styling support or related fiber management. It may be used on damp or dry hair and may be a spray, mist, milk, lotion, cream or two-phase system.

4.2 Product-form boundary rule
CLASSIFY BY FUNCTION + DIRECTIONS + ARCHITECTURE
Do not classify from the name alone. If conditioning/detangling is primary and the product remains on the hair, include it. If durable hold/texture is primary, route it to styling. If it is anhydrous oil/silicone, route it to oil/serum. If genuinely ambiguous, mark provisional and use it as a boundary stress case.

7. Core scientific research questions
Which formula architectures distinguish sprays, milks, creams, emulsions and two-phase leave-ins?
How should applied dose and expected grams per use affect INCI interpretation and weight potential?
Which cationic surfactants and polymers remain substantive in leave-on use, and how do they interact with anionic styling polymers?
How should volatile versus non-volatile silicones, amino silicones and silicone emulsions influence dry-down, shine, smoothing and persistence?
Which oils, esters, hydrocarbons, butters and solvents materially affect spread, slip, greasiness and transfer?
Which film-forming polymers support flexible hold, definition, humidity resistance or stiffness?
When do humectants support softness, and when is climate/humidity evidence required before making frizz claims?
Which proteins or polymers plausibly support lubrication, surface film, heat protection or breakage reduction—and which are weak hero ingredients?
What formula evidence can support heat protection, and which claims require product-specific thermal testing?
What predicts wash-out ease, cumulative buildup and need for clarification?
Which tests support wet combing, dry combing, friction, breakage, gloss, frizz, humidity response, curl retention and thermal protection?
Which conclusions can be made from formula alone, and which should default to unknown?

16. Definition of done
Category boundary is explicit and repeatable across spray, cream, styling and serum edge cases.
Dominant leave-in architectures and persistence behavior are documented from authoritative sources.
Claims are split into distinct mechanisms, including hold versus conditioning and heat evidence.
Direct product properties are operationally defined and separate from user-fit outputs.
Every score has gates, anchors, false-signal guidance and confidence limitations.
The lean matching profile is small enough for the current application model.
Every stored label has its own evidence record and counter-signals.
Ten to twelve exact German-market products have been classified and independently reviewed.
Three to five adversarial products have tested the weak points.
Unresolved source, category or proprietary-technology cases produce provisional records.
A runtime prompt produces both research_trace and matching_profile outputs for a newly uploaded product.
WHAT NOT TO OPTIMIZE FOR
Do not aim for a giant ontology on the first pass. Aim for reliable direct product behavior, a compact explainable matching profile and explicit unknowns for heat, humidity, layering and tolerance when formula-only reasoning is insufficient.
