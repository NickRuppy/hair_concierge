The patterns behind the proposal

The two-state design is not invented — it composes four established loading-UX rules:

Delay + minimum display

An indicator that appears and disappears within ~100–500 ms reads as a glitch. Once shown, progress UI must stay for a minimum beat so it never blinks — here the loading state holds for at least ~1.2 s even when the link resolves in 300 ms.

Pencil & Paper: Loading feedback OpenReplay: Loading indicators
Morph, don't swap

State changes land better when one element transforms than when content is replaced. Here the spinner arc literally becomes the success mark: the ring closes into a full circle, shifts plum → green, and the check draws itself inside it (SVG stroke-dashoffset). The headline never swaps text — two layers crossfade in the same reserved space.

Villar: Payment animations NN/g: Purpose of motion
Choreograph, don't fire at once

Carbon and Material motion guidance: exits use ease-in, entrances decelerate (cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)), siblings enter staggered rather than simultaneously, and nothing may shift layout — every element's space is reserved before it fades in. The sequence here: ring closes (450 ms) → check draws (300 ms) → headline crossfades → list and CTA rise in at 100 ms offsets, overlapping, ~700 ms total.

Say what the wait is for

Waits of 1–3 s deserve words, not bare spinners — and a moment of visible “preparation” after a purchase adds perceived value rather than friction. Hence “Dein Plan wird geöffnet.” instead of silence, and the extra reassurance line only when the wait is genuinely long.

Smart Interface Design Patterns

What the two-state design touches

  • No backend or routing changes. The replay-safe design stays exactly as is: read-only first render, the client POST /plan-bereit/status still does the linking. Only presentation changes.
  • welcome-client.tsx (redirect branch only) — the “Weiterleitung…” view becomes the loading frame: cream shell, wordmark, spinner, “Zahlung bestätigt”, “Dein Plan wird geöffnet.” The password/magic-link setup path for unauthenticated buyers is untouched.
  • plan-bereit/loading.tsx — replaced by the identical loading frame, so the streaming gap shows the same pixels.
  • personal-plan-ready-client.tsx — the checking state renders the same loading frame; on ready it morphs in place (spinner → check, headline crossfade, list + CTA staggered reveal) after a minimum beat. Error, retry, missing-fact and timeout states keep their current screens and copy, appearing only after the beat.
  • plan-ready-arrival.tsx — becomes the ready-state of that layout instead of a separate component swap.
  • Reduced motion: with prefers-reduced-motion, morph and reveals become instant switches after the same beat.
