# Evidence memo: Föhn on high heat - is Hitzeschutz needed? (2026-10-09)
Claim type: hair-fiber / routine (cosmetic, not medically adjacent). Sources: peer-reviewed lab studies; abstracts/excerpts read, full texts not all checked.

## Verdict
Overall confidence in "a plain Föhn needs heat protectant": LOW. Confidence that "heat/proximity affects cuticle wear somewhat": MODERATE.
Recommendation: (B), with Heiß -> "optional" at most. Do NOT use "empfohlen" for any Föhn setting.

## 1. Temperature that reaches the hair (rating: WEAK)
- Lee Y et al. 2011, Ann Dermatol 23(4):455-462: dryer air measured 0.5 cm from tress was 47 C at 15 cm, 61 C at 10 cm, 95 C at 5 cm. Hair temperature itself was NOT measured.
- Kamath & Petrovichova 2004, J Cosmet Sci 55(5):496-497 (abstract only): dryer at 15 cm, 80 C air; cortex temperature depends on conductivity and exposure time. Results not in the abstract.
- Dryer outlet air >100 C on high settings is plausible (patent examples 105-140 C; one J Cosmet Sci snippet, citation unverified). Wet hair stays cooler via evaporation (qualitative, physics + patents), heats up as it dries.
- I found no peer-reviewed measurement of hair-surface temperature at highest setting and typical distance. TRI (trade summary) says dryers are <70 C and "not really an issue" - not a primary source.
- Context: flat irons >200 C (Zhou 2011); dry-hair irreversible change from ~180-200 C, wet hair from ~160 C (TRI summary of Popescu). Air-dryer hair temps are well below this range, but no single study compares them.

## 2. Damage from blow-drying; heat vs water (rating: MODERATE for existence, WEAK for mechanism split)
- Lee 2011: 30 shampoo+dry cycles on virgin tresses. Cuticle cracks at 47 C, more lifting at 61 C, holes/punched-out cuticle only at 95 C. NO cortex damage in any group. Moisture differences n.s. In vitro, n per group unreported, one dryer, drying time differed per group (tresses dried to completion).
- Same study: air drying (>2 h wet contact) was the only group with cell-membrane-complex damage; authors conclude dryer at 15 cm, moving, caused LESS overall damage than air drying. So "nothing" is not the damage-free baseline.
- Gamez-Garcia 1998, J Cosmet Sci 49:141-153: repeated wet/blow-dry cycles make axial cuticle cracks; mechanism = rapid dehydration of outer cuticle (water-driven stress), not simple overheating.
- Christian P et al. 2011, J Cosmet Sci 62:15-27 (flat iron, not dryer): heat does similar chemical damage wet or dry, but wet hair gets much more structural damage; rapid water evaporation is the likely cause.
- Zi Y et al. 2025, J Cosmet Dermatol 24(8):e70360: 30 wet blow-dries, highest setting, 10 cm, 3 min/side = lifted cuticle, contact angle 129.6 -> 92.4 deg, tryptophan 17.0 -> 13.6 mg/kg; break work and water content n.s. vs oven model. In vitro, single hair type, no protectant tested.
- Net: cumulative, mostly cuticle-level; rises with air temperature/proximity; wet-state evaporation also contributes. Heat vs water share NOT established (no strong consensus).

## 3. Protectant efficacy under blow-drying (rating: NONE)
- Zhou Y et al. 2011, J Cosmet Sci 62:265-282 (ISP, manufacturer-affiliated): polymer pretreatments (e.g. VP/acrylates/lauryl methacrylate copolymer, polyquaternium-55) cut breakage and hair temperature - FLAT IRON only; blow dryers mentioned only in the intro. Christian 2011 likewise flat iron. TRI: spray reduces but does not prevent damage at 232 C.
- No peer-reviewed study found testing a heat protectant under blow-drying conditions. Transfer from flat-iron data is an extrapolation. Consumer claims ("reduces heat exposure by up to 50%", "2022 meta-analysis") could not be traced; ignore.

## 4. Special cases
- Round brush / tension (rating: WEAK, indirect): Ruetsch & Kamath 2004, J Cosmet Sci (curling iron ~110-120 C): wet hair under 10-30 g tension -> cuticle disintegration, cracking, steam bubbling; dry, low-tension, short-duration = minimal damage. Gamez-Garcia 1997 preprint links blow-dry + combing to cuticle chipping. No study on round-brush dryer sessions. Bubble hair case reports (Int J Trichology) after blow-drying damp hair: mechanism plausible, prevalence unknown. Practical lever = dry the hair mostly before brushing, avoid heat on soaking-wet hair under tension (supported mechanism, weak direct data).
- Diffuser (rating: NONE): no measured study of diffuser vs nozzle damage. Spreading airflow lowers local intensity (mechanistic claim only); distance/contact with scalp and hair can offset it. A diffuser does NOT change the verdict: treat like Föhn. Warmluftbürste/Airstyler (heat + tension + proximity) staying "optional" is consistent.

## Conflicts / gaps
- TRI "no detectable damage" vs Lee 2011 and Zi 2025 (measurable cuticle changes after 30 cycles). Both can hold: cuticle wear is real, strength loss is small.
- Lee's 15 cm / moving / air-dry comparison is one in vitro study; do not hard-code distances.
- Unknown: real hair-surface temperature, effect of hair type (bleached/fine hair is more heat-vulnerable per Kamath-era TRI work), real-world frequency.

## Product recommendation (conservative)
- Choose (B): ask temperature only for users who föhnen; Kalt/Mittel -> "nicht nötig"; Heiß -> "optional" (never "empfohlen"). Mittel is not raised, because evidence at 47-61 C shows only minor cracking and no protectant benefit data.
- Optional refinement: raise the Föhn note when the profile already shows bleached/colored or damaged hair, or when the user also uses a round brush on wet hair. Keep this a soft hint ("kann helfen, Beleg schwach"), not a rule.
- Behavior tips outrank products: lower setting, keep dryer moving, ~15 cm distance, partly towel/air dry first, avoid brushing soaking-wet hair under tension. Copy must not claim Hitzeschutz "schützt vor Föhnschäden"; say damage is mainly cuticle wear and protectant benefit for Föhn is unproven.
- Acceptable fallback (A) if the question slot is costly: the question's only product consequence is an "optional" label, so low value; value mainly lies in the behavior tip.
- Diffuser: no separate branch.
- Keep out: any numeric temperature thresholds for hair-damage in user copy; "%" protection claims.

## Open risks
- Optional-label still implies a benefit that is unproven; wording must say "geringer Nutzen belegt".
- Follow-up research: hair-surface temperature during highest-setting blow-drying (IR/thermocouple), protectant vs. none under dryer-only cycling, round-brush tension tests. Verify full texts of Lee 2011 and Ruetsch & Kamath 2004 citation details before citing in public copy.
