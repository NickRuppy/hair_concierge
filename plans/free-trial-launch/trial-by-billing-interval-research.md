# Free trials by billing interval

Checked 12 September 2026. Planning evidence; no checkout completed, account created, provider setting changed, or product decision changed.

## Question and method

Does selecting monthly versus annual change whether a free trial is available, and its duration? Compare 12 named consumer subscription apps from the ongoing competitor discussion. This is a purposive comparison, not a representative sample or a verified ranking of highest-performing apps.

Count a trial against an interval only when documentation or an observed offer connects the two. An annual-selected screenshot does not prove that monthly lacks a trial. Unknown means unverified, not unavailable. Paid introductory periods and non-renewing free previews are excluded. Terms can vary by channel, region, account eligibility and experiment; the table describes the specified offer, not every offer from that company.

## Counts and limits

Of these 12 apps:

- **3** document the same trial on monthly and annual: Fitbod, MyFitnessPal, Headway (seven days each).
- **1** explicitly documents different trial lengths on both: Headspace (seven versus fourteen).
- **1** offers trials on both in the inspected web flow, but monthly duration remains unverified: Strava.
- **1** officially documents annual-only trial enrollment in its standard in-app flow: Calm.
- **1** has an annual-only trial in a recent third-party account report: Duolingo. Keep this weaker evidence separate from official-policy counts.
- **5** cannot be fully classified: Cal AI, Blinkist, AllTrails, Noom, RiseGuide.

Thus five have evidence of trials on both intervals; two show annual-only trial offers if the account-specific Duolingo report is included; five remain unresolved. This does not establish which approach most apps use or which earns most money.

The public [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps) distinguishes trial-only, no-trial and mixed offers. Its published category breakdown does not identify the exact monthly-versus-annual trial split requested here. Mixed offers cannot be equated with annual-only trials.

## Implication for Chaarlie

There are separate choices: whether monthly gets a trial at all, and whether its duration matches annual. Seven days on both has direct competitor precedents; annual-only trial enrollment is also a real approach. Neither is proven to maximize Chaarlie revenue by this comparison.
