## Deterministic selection contract

1. Run existing category fit/identity/safety authorities first. Never relax a failure for price or tier. Candidate commerce is usable only with a finite positive EUR package price, usable purchase URL/status, paired positive quantity/unit and reviewed evidence that those fields describe the **same selected purchasable package**. Structural completeness alone is not exact-package proof. Valid last-known prices remain usable without an age cutoff; show their date. Unknown/invalid commerce is not counted as affordable, an expensive upgrade or an eligible priced recommendation.
2. Build authority-backed required roles and optional roles separately. Necessary gaps are only required purchases with suitable verified candidates but no suitable affordable candidate; empty optional cards are not gaps. If even an expensive suitable candidate is missing, show the existing truthful unavailable/recovery state rather than inventing a gap fill.
3. Count `N` as unique product IDs to be purchased in the final proposed portfolio, including preserved planned purchases; exclude owned products, displayed alternatives and empty slots. A product used for multiple roles counts once. Do not add items to improve a ratio.
4. Capped-strict: never automatically select above the ceiling. Count suitable affordable alternatives over the **full pool**. At least two: display affordable choices only. One/zero: show suitable above-budget choices directly with a label and any affordable choice first; with zero, leave selection empty. Explicit manual above-budget selection is permitted and never changes the saved budget.
5. Capped-flexible: ordinary allowance `A = N === 0 ? 0 : max(1, floor(0.25 * N))`. Necessary gaps `G` have priority; capacity is `max(A, G)` and ordinary automatic exceptions have at most `max(0, A - G)` places. Manual selections may exceed the allowance; preserve them, count them for the routine-wide accounting, and do not mutate preferences or add another approval modal.
6. An ordinary exception compares against the best suitable affordable candidate for the **same category and role**. It must remove a documented authority-level compromise for an important confirmed need, introduce no new relevant compromise and not worsen overall fit. More badges, rendered matches, prestige or tier alone are insufficient. No affordable baseline means the necessary-gap rules, not an ordinary improvement claim.
7. After gaps, qualifying upgrades addressing the explicitly confirmed main concern take priority. Map versioned source concern IDs to versioned authority criterion IDs; never infer a main concern from arbitrary labels. Only comparable improvements use lower `extraCost` over their own baseline as the next tie-break. Equal evidence/cost uses existing deterministic authority/catalog/ID order. No invented cross-category numerical benefit score.
8. Explicit tier preference is a budget-compatible tie-break among fit-comparable candidates, with explained fallback. It cannot override strict price limits, fit/safety or necessary role identity. Labels apply initially to regular Shampoo, Conditioner and Mask, not Leave-in/Oil or unresolved deep-cleansing rows.

Authority mapping should use underlying criteria (for example Conditioner weight/balance/repair and Mask weight/care direction/repair support), excluding aggregate summary criteria so the same evidence is not counted twice. Implement and review the finite concern→criterion mapping from actual Stage-1 IDs; unsupported evidence must not be promoted into a proven upgrade.

Final portfolio validation recomputes unique `N`, `G` and consumed places after cross-role deduplication. If deduplication shrinks `A`, remove the lowest-priority **automatic ordinary** exceptions until valid; never remove a necessary gap fill, an owned product or an explicit choice. Re-evaluate the affected role against its affordable baseline and validate again. The bounded number of automatic exceptions guarantees termination; no extra items are created.
