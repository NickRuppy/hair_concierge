- No mandatory tier-preference onboarding question. An explicitly expressed tier preference can be a budget-compatible tie-break among equally suitable options, with explained other-bucket fallback; it never overrides fit/safety or strict price limits.

### Selected simple inference rules — point 30

Use unique exact matched hair-care consumables; exclude tools and known duplicates. Unknown matches/prices count against coverage, not as silently dropped evidence. Use reviewed canonical selected-package prices as orientation, not a claim about what the user paid; no new age-only price cutoff.

Require at least **3 priceable distinct products**, at least **2 categories**, and **≥80% priceable coverage** of entered consumables. Then:

- Suggest €5 if ≥80% of priceable products cost ≤€5, with **none >€15**.
- Suggest €15 if ≥80% cost **>€5 and ≤€15**, with **none >€15**.
- Otherwise no suggestion.

Examples: 8/10/14 → €15; 3/4/5 → €5; 4/4/25 → no selection. Do not require every price to be ≤€5 for the low-band case; do not count low prices toward the middle-band share. These are selected working rules, not open confidence-score/model questions. Exact evidence/package binding still needs technical hardening.

### Recommendation rules

1. Existing fit, safety, identity and role/category authority run first. Tier has no fit bonus. Never use marketing, recommendation frequency or raw displayed match counts as proof of an expensive upgrade.
2. **Capped strict:** no automatic above-cap selection. Count suitable affordable candidates over the full eligible pool. With ≥2, show affordable only. With 1 or 0, show higher-priced suitable alternatives directly and clearly labelled, affordable first where present. With 0, nothing is preselected. No “show expensive options” reveal step or fake empty card slots. Manual above-budget choice is allowed without changing the saved preference.
3. **Capped flexible:** ordinary exception allowance `A = N === 0 ? 0 : max(1, floor(0.25 * N))`. `N` is actual unique new purchases in the final proposed portfolio, including preserved planned purchases, not all routine roles, owned products, displayed alternatives or empty slots. Cross-role duplicate product IDs count once.
4. Necessary required-purchase gaps `G` take priority. Capacity is `max(A, G)`; ordinary upgrades have `max(0, A - G)` places. A necessary gap means suitable verified products exist but none suitable/affordable for a required role. No suitable verified product at any price is truthful unavailable recovery, not an invented gap. No extra euro ceiling, extra AI model or budget-permission modal.
5. Ordinary expensive exception must remove an authority-backed concrete compromise for an important confirmed user need against the **best suitable affordable baseline for the same role**, introduce no relevant new compromise and not worsen overall fit. Equal fit/prestige alone is not enough. Unclear improvement → keep affordable automatically; manual alternative remains available.
6. After gaps, upgrades addressing the explicitly confirmed **main concern** take priority. Only equally relevant/comparable gains use lower extra cost over their own baseline as tie-break; then stable existing authority/catalog/ID order. Do not invent a cross-category numeric benefit score or main concern.
7. An allowed expensive exception can be the primary recommendation directly, with price/reason and an affordable swap where available. No extra per-exception consent step. User can exchange it.
8. Explicit manual choices are preserved even when exceeding the automatic share. Do not change the preference, undo the choice or add purchases to repair a ratio.
9. **No ceiling:** selected **working soft target** is approximately 60% of new purchases >€15 and 40% ≤€15, with `round(0.60 * N)` high-price count. Fit/safety/supply win; never add items or select worse matches to force the ratio. Five purchases illustrate 3/2. This is not a final launch-approved allocator or a required Profi share.

- Existing users may view routines without interruption.
