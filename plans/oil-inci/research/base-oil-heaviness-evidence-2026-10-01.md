# Base oils and expected heaviness: evidence review

Reviewed 2026-10-01. Research requested by Nick after the conditioner/leave-in weight comparison. This is a source-backed comparison and a proposal for further calibration, not an adopted weight rule. It changes no frozen round, dictionary, product classification, fit policy or production record.

## Conclusion

Light hair-oil formulas are plausible and explicitly offered by ingredient manufacturers. Fast-spreading ester/alkane systems provide the clearest candidates. The reviewed public evidence does not establish an equally clear, universal ranking of neat botanical oils by perceived heaviness on hair.

Castor is a strong candidate for the richer end because its unusually viscous flow is demonstrated. Coconut has a temperature-dependent solid/liquid transition and direct evidence of changing surface film with time. Neither density nor penetration is a hair-weight classification. The familiar claim that coconut is universally heavier than argan, jojoba or grapeseed is not established by this review.

## What the sources actually measure

- **Density:** mass per volume. It changes the mass delivered at a fixed volume, but supplies no validated threshold for limpness, greasiness or coating.
- **Viscosity:** resistance to flow, strongly dependent on temperature and grade. It can inform distribution; it does not determine the feel of retained material on hair.
- **Spreading/sensory:** a useful material distinction when its substrate and endpoint are recorded. Skin dry-feel data remain skin evidence.
- **Surface film and penetration:** hair experiments can show redistribution over time. That is not a measurement of light/medium/rich, nor repeated-use build-up.
- **Finished formula:** dose, companions and residual film matter. A fast-spreading base can carry substantial nonvolatile gums/oils. Conversely, a botanical hero need not be the carrier.

## Brief comparison for engine development

The expectations below are proposed qualitative priors, not measured classes. Alternatives are intentionally retained; they are not new enum values.

| Base material | Working expectation | Evidence and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Castor oil, `Ricinus Communis Seed Oil` | Rich candidate; stronger material basis than the other botanical rankings | Primary rheology separates castor from the more fluid jojoba/olive systems [S1]. The move from viscosity to hair richness is still an estimate, not a sensory result. Modified castor polymers and PEG derivatives are separate materials. |
| Whole coconut oil, `Cocos Nucifera Oil` | Medium/rich candidate; retain the existing rich result as a provisional estimate pending comparison | Primary thermal measurements show melting near ordinary room temperature [S2]. Hair surface film thins with time in the reviewed experiment [S3]. Neither establishes that coconut always weighs hair down more than another oil. |
| Jojoba, `Simmondsia Chinensis Seed Oil` | Medium starting estimate; possible lighter botanical comparator | Jojoba is a wax-ester oil, with a more fluid rheology than castor [S1]. No reviewed head-to-head hair-weight test establishes a light class. |
| Argan, `Argania Spinosa Kernel Oil` | Medium starting estimate, low confidence | Included as a benchmark in supplier hair work [S5], which does not publish a ranking of heaviness against the other neat botanical oils. Do not import lightweight retail positioning as formula truth. |
| Grapeseed, sunflower, sweet almond | Medium starting estimates; candidates for testing a lighter botanical end | Grapeseed/sunflower have direct physical-property measurements [S4, S6]; almond appears in a supplier's lightweight *blend* [S7]. These observations do not prove neat-oil lightness or a species ranking on hair. |
| Olive and avocado | Medium starting estimates; richer alternatives remain plausible | Olive has primary rheology and hair-film observations [S1, S3]; avocado is among the measured liquid oils [S6]. This review does not establish automatic rich membership from either botanical name. |
| Defined caprylic/capric triglyceride material | Light/medium candidate, grade-sensitive | Inolex describes a particular grade as lightweight and uses it in a hair oil [S7, S8]. Its exact product page describes a majority of eight-carbon chains; the prototype page instead says six. Treat that as a documentation conflict, not proof of grade variation. Do not equate the processed material with whole coconut oil. |
| Reviewed fast-spreading esters and alkanes | Strong candidates for light | Supplier hair prototype and exact-material references support this route [S7, S9–S12]. Examples include C13-15 Alkane, Triheptanoin/C13-16 Isoalkane, Coco-Caprylate and Dicaprylyl Carbonate. Fast spreading is not proof of evaporation or low build-up. |
| Silicone or mineral-oil systems | Grade/formula dependent; no family-wide class | Momentive separates light volatile carriers, non-greasy fluids and gum-carrying blends [S11]. Mineral oil's retained surface film differs from the plant oils tested [S3]; that does not establish its perceived heaviness. |

## Source register and transfer ceilings

**S1 — primary material rheology.** Ghannam et al., 2023, [Flow characteristics of jojoba and other oil blends for skin treatment fluids](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/flow-characteristics-of-jojoba-and-other-oil-blends-for-skin-trea/), DOI 10.1016/j.ijft.2023.100362. Author-institution abstract reviewed. Measurements cover density, viscosity and rheology over temperatures; jojoba/olive and castor/glycerin form different flow profiles. No hair sensory endpoint.

**S2 — primary thermal experiment.** [Edible Oils as Practical Phase Change Materials for Thermal Energy Storage](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/8/1627), 2019. Indexed experiment/table text reviewed: tested virgin and refined coconut materials melt around 24°C. Raw-material phase behavior, not hair richness or finished-formula melting.

**S3 — primary hair experiment, abstract reviewed.** Keis et al., 2006, [Investigation of penetration abilities of various oils into human hair fibers](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-2494.2006.00304_4.x). Coconut, olive and sunflower-treated fibres showed declining capillary adhesion with time; mineral did not. Optical work supported thinning surface films. Do not convert this into a heaviness score or universal penetration rule.

**S4 — primary physical-property experiment.** [Rapid adulteration detection of cold pressed oils with their refined versions by UV–Vis spectroscopy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7528079/), 2020. Indexed full-text table/methods reviewed; direct PMC opening was challenged. Density comparisons do not establish a coconut-heavy ordering. Coconut measurements use a different temperature; the displayed viscosity units/magnitudes merit checking before numerical reuse. No numerical comparison is adopted here.

**S5 — supplier hair research summary.** Croda, [Oil evaluation on hair](https://www.crodabeauty.com/en-gb/resources/blog/oil-evaluation-on-hair), 2026-02-11. Benchmarks include jojoba, argan, olive, sunflower and a silicone. The public page reports other endpoints, not a complete dose-controlled neat-oil heaviness comparison; fuller material requires login. Its avocado/olive-derived butter is not interchangeable with neat avocado oil.

**S6 — primary rheology experiment.** Diamante and Lan, 2014, [Absolute Viscosities of Vegetable Oils at Different Temperatures and Shear Rate Range](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/2014/234583). Abstract and accessible article text reviewed; liquid-oil viscosity changes with temperature. This is food/material testing, not hair sensory classification.

**S7 — supplier finished prototype.** Inolex, [Hair Oil HC-1112](https://www.inolex.com/prototypes/hair-oil). Published silicone-free blend described as lightweight: 25% Triheptanoin/C13-16 Isoalkane, 20% C13-15 Alkane, 21.5% a caprylic/capric triglyceride grade, with almond/hemp/argan and fragrance. Concrete formulation evidence that a light oil concept is feasible; public page lacks a controlled hair-weight comparison. Those disclosed amounts describe this prototype only and must never be inferred for retail products.

**S8 — supplier exact grade.** Inolex, [Lexol GT-865 MB](https://www.inolex.com/products/lexol-mb-gt-865). Exact INCI Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, lightweight and low-viscosity description. The current product page's eight-carbon statement conflicts with the prototype's six-carbon statement; the detailed chain distribution is not adopted. No universal caprylic/capric triglyceride lightness rule.

**S9 — supplier exact ester.** BASF, [Cetiol C 5](https://care360.basf.com/global/en/Product-Finder/30538241), exact INCI Coco-Caprylate. Fast spreading; silicone-alternative positioning does not establish volatility. Supporting technical information is skin/personal-care oriented, not a measured retail hair-weight score.

**S10 — supplier exact carbonate.** BASF, [Cetiol CC](https://care360.basf.com/emea/en/industries/personal-care/concepts-trends/products/products-detail-highlight/30527869), Dicaprylyl Carbonate; fast spreading/dry sensory description includes hair applications. No automatic light classification for all esters.

**S11 — supplier hair materials.** Momentive, [Beauty and Personal Care Product Overview, June 2024](https://www.momentive.com/content/dam/momentive/global/sub-category-landing-pages/additves/beauty---personal-care-additives---sub-categories/beauty---personal-care-additives---emulsifiers-and-dispersants/files/Personal_Care_General_Overview_JUNE2024%20%281%29.pdf), PDF page 5 / printed pages 8–9. Separates C9-12 Alkane and Ethyl Trisiloxane carriers from nonvolatile shine materials and silicone-gum blends. Grade descriptions are supplier evidence, not blind retail classifications. Do not normalize contradictory C13-15/C13-16 entries without checking exact material documentation.

**S12 — supplier exact ester/alkane blend, hair sensory claims.** Inolex, [LexFeel WOW](https://www.inolex.com/products/lexfeel-wow), Triheptanoin and C13-16 Isoalkane. Public material page describes fast spreading, non-greasy afterfeel and comparable hair-serum sensory to D5. This is explicit hair-relevant supplier support for a light candidate, without a public controlled test protocol or independent replication. It does not establish that every formula containing either INCI is light.

## Proposed next step

Keep `light | medium | rich | unknown`. Add source-backed material priors to a future dictionary revision; use them within the whole-formula judgment, with grade, temperature and dose limitations. Do not adopt the leave-in engine's rich botanical enumeration as externally validated oil science.

For a deliberately contrasting next calibration set, capture exact DE/EU products representing: pure castor, whole coconut, pure jojoba, pure grapeseed or argan, a defined alkane-led light candidate, and a fast-spreading ester-led candidate. Separate whole coconut from fractionated/modified material. Freeze inputs and run blind classification before revealing lightweight marketing. These researched candidates are calibration cases, not unseen holdout cases. Do not tune thresholds merely to make every class appear.

Acceptance: the procedure must explain both light and rich candidates without equating fluidity with hair weight, automatically treating plant oils as rich, or automatically treating persistent material as medium. Keep the initial round and its disagreements intact; later policy adoption needs synchronized standard/guide changes and a new derivation.
