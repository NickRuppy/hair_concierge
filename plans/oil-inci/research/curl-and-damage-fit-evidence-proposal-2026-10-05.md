# Oil curl and damage fit — evidence and next decisions

Date: 2026-10-05. Status: **research and proposal only; no curl/damage rule adopted**. Nick authorized finalizing thickness fit and continuing these two axes. External evidence was reviewed independently of internal methodology, followed by explicitly labeled reconciliation. No product property, weight, repair/protein/moisture direction or production eligibility is changed.

## Answer-first recommendation

First decision: **do not add a separate oil-weight ladder or exclusion from curl pattern alone**. Keep the settled thickness/weight preferences, intended use and desired finish independent. For example, fine coily hair keeps its fine-hair load caveat; coarse straight hair keeps its coarse-hair finish preference. Curl pattern is not a substitute for strand diameter.

Possible alternative: a soft straight/wavy-to-curly/coily heavier-finish preference. This is recognizable professional shorthand and may make personalization more visible, but oil-specific evidence does not calibrate such a ladder independently of diameter, density, dose and desired styling result. It would need to be an explicitly chosen heuristic, with counterexamples and no curl-definition promise. It is not recommended for the initial oil engine.

If the neutral-axis proposal is accepted, keep all four pattern cells as no additional pattern exclusion for an established fibre role; label this as compatibility policy rather than measured performance or curl-definition benefit. The precise emission convention belongs to that decision, not to this evidence review. Scalp-only scenarios still cannot acquire fibre fit.

Second decision, after pattern: whether damage adds an oil-selection preference. Recommendation: **no automatic damage-to-weight or damage-to-penetration bonus/penalty**. Surface lubrication and penetrating treatments can both be relevant. Studies do not validate the engine's healthy/moderate/high categories as a ranking scale. A narrow protective pre-wash suggestion based on exact ingredient/regimen evidence is more defensible than a universal damaged-hair fit score; do not create that additional suggestion field without an explicit decision.

## External hair evidence

| Source and evidence tier | Actual endpoint/context | Permitted interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Rele and Mohile, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12715094/), primary controlled hair study | Coconut, sunflower and mineral oils on multiple Indian-origin hair preparations, including undamaged and experimentally damaged hair; pre/post-shampoo treatments, wet-combing proteinaceous debris and water-retention measurements. Long treatment times and neat-oil protocols differ from ordinary finishing-serum application. | Coconut protection under specific treatment conditions is plausible for pre-wash interpretation. The study does not define three damage-fit tiers, curl definition, general finished-blend efficacy or a protein-treatment classification. Industry-origin research; scope its endpoint. |
| [Marsh et al., 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ics.12978), primary penetration/fatigue study | Coconut, camellia and safflower oils and isolated triglycerides on untreated/bleached purchased hair, including 24 hours at 40 C, differential extraction and NanoSIMS. Penetration was greater in virgin than bleached hair. Greater coconut penetration did not establish significantly greater fatigue benefit than the other tested oils. | Neither more damage implies more uptake nor more penetration implies more benefit is a defensible generic rule. The exact treatment and solvent-removal conditions limit retail-use transfer. |
| [Lourenco et al., 2024, textured hair](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9284/11/6/212), primary neat-oil study | Purchased Type IV textured tresses, virgin or bleached; argan/avocado/coconut at 1 g/g hair for 24 hours followed by SLES wash; imaging and mechanical tests. Mechanical findings varied by oil and damage. | Study taxonomy is not automatically equivalent to the engine's coily label. Mixed results cannot support a universal curl exclusion, penetrating-oil benefit ladder or normal-dose finished-oil judgment. External reviewer inspected methods/results; main publisher open was rate-limited. |
| [Kim and Ahn, 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40691-023-00332-0), primary wash-off treatment study | Strongly bleached hair, repeated wash-off creams containing individual fatty acids, MCT or coconut oil; lipid and mechanical/protein endpoints. | Formulation/treatment-dependent effects, not an anhydrous finishing-oil trial. Free fatty acids do not substitute for triglyceride identity. No validated healthy/moderate/high matrix. |
| [Effects of Cosmetic Emulsions on Mongolian Hair, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8991896/), primary formulated-emulsion study | Virgin and bleached purchased hair; rinsed formulations with cationic/fatty-alcohol conditioning systems plus dimethicone or an ester, with optional glyceryl oleate. Friction, wetting and combing endpoints. | Surface conditioning is a legitimate benefit route for damaged hair. Complete-formula differences matter. This does not prove every surface-led oil's benefit. External reviewer inspected the study; main PMC open returned a browser challenge. |
| [AAD curly-hair guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/curly-hair-care) and [AAD leave-in guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/leave-in-conditioner-tips/), professional practice | Supports conditioning curly/coily hair, optional oil after washing/conditioning and adapting amounts/products to actual needs. | Supports need-based use, not a validated oil-weight ladder independent of diameter. General conditioner richness must not become a hard oil-class exclusion. |

Open evidence risks: finished formulas at ordinary doses, independently varied pattern versus diameter, a validated definition of moderate versus high damage, and exact-formula benefit comparisons. None of these gaps is proof that pattern/damage never matter. Preserve cosmetic scope and avoid structural repair, growth, curl hold/definition or efficacy claims.

## Internal guidance, separately mapped

- Oil property vocabulary owns thickness fine/normal/coarse, pattern straight/wavy/curly/coily, and damage healthy/moderately_damaged/highly_damaged. Pattern and damage policy are still undefined. Penetration is a formula-architecture estimate, not measured finished-product action; surface-led does not mean zero penetration. Repair and protein/moisture direction are explicitly out.
- Conditioner standard v1.6's profile derivation at lines 207–272 keeps thickness and texture distinct. Its damage matrix depends on conditioning and category-specific repair/protection paths; generic oil is not sufficient. These cannot be imported into the oil engine as oil-fit rules.
- Leave-In standard v1.0, lines 1462–1509, has its own weight/slip/conditioning/repair-based matrices; lines 1596–1609 distinguish curl-definition focus from oil heaviness alone. Neither its texture matrix nor its highly-damaged gate is inherited here.
- Both category methods support transferable safeguards: formula truth precedes fit, no naked efficacy label, scalp permission is separate, and published route/dose/context are retained.
