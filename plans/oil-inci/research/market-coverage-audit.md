## Property-definition gaps to resolve

1. Base lists six candidate values but four effect descriptions. Hybrids and ester/alkane-rich blends need honest boundaries; esters are not automatically volatile. Avoid fixed top-three/top-five cutoffs before calibration.
2. Named-oil "share" should mean rank/prominence, not quantity. Handle no named oil, multiple named oils, absent advertised oil, and source conflict. Hero selection happens after unblinding; formula architecture/weight remain blind-first.
3. Penetration evidence must distinguish studies on specific oils from an inference about a finished blend. Coconut alkanes are not interchangeable with coconut triglyceride oil. Neither a trade name nor a leading-oil rule is scientific proof; science pass pending.
4. Build-up needs bounded persistence/removal language and unknown handling. Ingredient-family presence alone must not become a universal washout prediction.
5. Botanical flags need exact INCI forms: rosemary leaf extract versus essential oil; individual botanical matches versus generic Parfum/allergens. Presence is not concentration, cooling magnitude, scalp suitability or intolerance.
6. Supported use needs a site/duration/rinse contract. Pre-wash scalp use alone must not create a verified pre-wash fibre-treatment role.
7. Thickness/curl/damage projections require separate justification. Absence of a repair property does not make all damage levels suitable by default.

## Conclusion and subsequent ruling

The original nine selected aspects remain a useful foundation, with toning excluded and scalp-active botanicals retained. The old survey does not prove complete coverage or equivalence of performance across base classes. UV was the strongest omitted protection direction; humidity and drying speed remain distinct claim/evidence topics.

Nick subsequently approved UV as the tenth aspect under OE12: true only when a recognized UV-filter ingredient and an explicit manufacturer hair UV-protection claim are both present; false otherwise. This supersedes the earlier proposed three-state treatment. False means the positive-evidence classification rule was not met, not tested absence of protection. Scientific rules for penetration, weight, persistence and scalp exposures remain a separate next research pass.

## Scope clarification and targeted science check

Nick clarified after this audit that the core engine reads INCI and classifies the formula. Marketing directions test coverage; they are not themselves candidate formula-derived characteristics. Exact-product heat claims may be adopted; UV is now included with the ingredient-plus-claim rule in OE12. Additional humidity/drying-speed/claim dimensions are not authorized by the marketing audit.

Separate three evidence types in the eventual output contract:

- Direct INCI observations: recognized ingredient identities/families, selected exposure flags and named-oil rank. No concentration or tolerance inference.
- Bounded formula judgments: base architecture, weight, penetration potential and persistence potential, with explicit anchors, evidence limits and unknown handling. Finished-product outcome claims cannot be relabelled as direct observations.
- Captured external facts: dispenser from pack; applications from exact directions; heat protection from an admissible manufacturer claim; UV claim paired with recognized INCI evidence under OE12. Each carries its source rather than pretending to come solely from INCI.

UV-filter presence and declared hair protection are different facts. [Li et al., 2016, abstract reviewed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26095391/) experimentally compared four filters on hair. Adsorption increased with concentration; measured protection varied by filter and endpoint. This supports identifying UV-absorbing ingredients while refusing to infer the commercial formula's protection level from presence alone. It is a mechanistic study, not validation of any surveyed product.

[2011 polymer-pretreatment study, abstract reviewed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21635854/) tested selected polymer pretreatments and measured keratin damage/breakage after hot flat ironing. Such ingredients provide mechanistic clues; the study does not establish a universal INCI-only heat-protection classifier. The proposed engine therefore retains manufacturer-declared heat protection as the explicitly accepted exception.

Recommended scope test for every proposed aspect: can it distinguish products from their formula with defensible evidence? If not, does an explicitly accepted claim, pack or directions fact justify capturing it? Otherwise omit it from classification, even if it is a common marketing direction.
