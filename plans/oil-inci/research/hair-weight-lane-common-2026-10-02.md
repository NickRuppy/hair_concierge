# Common oils: hair-weight evidence lane

This lane covers the first sixteen exact materials from `natural-oil-weight-v0.5.json`. The recommendation is a deliberately approximate tendency at equal, modest, neat leave-on dose on hair. It predicts relative surface-film/volume burden, not absorption into skin, therapeutic benefit, ingredient quality, or the sensory result of a silicone/emulsion blend.

The JSON companion records an exact source URL, a verified observation, and whether each inference is hair-specific guidance, direct hair measurement, or material inference. None of this set has a direct controlled perceived-weight comparison. Coconut and sunflower hair-fibre studies support film/adhesion or damage mechanisms only; they cannot decide greasy feel, volume, or a neat leave-on rank. Olive's class uses its independently reported 70-90 mPa s range, not capillary adhesion. Hemp is light from a 20-50-cP 25 C physicochemical study; refined baobab is light from an exact 25-40-cps material specification. Both remain low-confidence material inferences. All skin-only descriptors, supplier blends, and rinse-out/pre-wash evidence are explicitly marked non-decisive in the JSON.

Access limits: supplier documents often bind sensory statements to a refined/organic grade, while plain INCI usually does not disclose grade, cultivar, or processing. No accessible controlled cross-oil neat leave-on human-hair weight/volume panel was found. These labels therefore require later blinded calibration before becoming a normative recommendation rule.
