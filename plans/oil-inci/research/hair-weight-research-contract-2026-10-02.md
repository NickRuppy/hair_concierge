Nick's latest request authorizes completing the research for all 64 collected materials and preparing the entire proposed reference for sign-off. It retains OE14's explicit authority for approximate estimates and OE21's correction: skin absorption is not hair weight. The complete proposed result is prepared before asking for a decision. The target is relative tendency to leave an oily coating, encourage strand clumping or reduce body after comparable modest leave-on application. It is not oil density, nutrient richness, shine, penetration, combing performance or skin absorption. Dose, hair diameter/damage, temperature, time, grade and other carriers can change a finished product's result.

When these are absent, use measured material flow/film properties or an explicitly qualified material-family extrapolation to propose a low-confidence hair-weight tendency. A numerical viscosity threshold, fatty-acid shortcut, arbitrary class quota, or medium solely because evidence is absent is not authorized. ## General hair evidence recovered

- Dobinson and Petter, **Sensory perception and evaluation of hair greasiness**, J. Soc. Cosmet. Chem. 27 (1976), 3–14. [Original article reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/240142089/Hair-Greasiness), original article text read, excluding the hosting site's generated summary. Equal deposited mass on hair switches; tactile and visual assessment. Higher viscosity tracked greater greasiness across four silicone liquids. Semi-solid and wax relationships differed. This supplies a hair-relevant rationale for considering rheology, not validation or class thresholds for botanical oils. The silicone series spans a much wider viscosity range than ordinary seed oils.
- Marsh, Knowles and Simmonds, P&G and Kew, **Choosing the Right Plant Oil for Hair and Skin Benefits** (2023). [Author technical article](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/magazine/article/22862759/cosmetics-toiletries-magazine-choosing-the-right-plant-oil-for-hair-and-skin-benefits), public full text read. Describes material flow, physical state and dose-dependent surface lubrication. Supports a transparent engineering inference; does not report a comparative botanical hair-heaviness panel.
- Unilever, [US20020039564A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20020039564A1/en), examples and panel endpoints read. Pure whole coconut was the hair greasiness benchmark and the tested hydrocarbon-containing blends felt less greasy. Patent evidence, with limited panel/protocol reporting. Supports a richer coconut tendency and the relevance of carrier architecture; cannot isolate the small amounts of other botanical oils or rank them.
- Keis et al., **Investigation of penetration abilities of various oils into human hair fibers**, J. Cosmet. Sci. 56 (2005), 283–295. [Original article PDF reproduction](https://1.oliveoiltimes.com/library/olive-oil-hair.pdf), methods/results read. Oil-film adhesion varies with time and heat; surface film is a relevant mechanism. This is not a perceived-weight ranking. Do not turn penetration into light, or assign olive rich from adhesion while assigning sunflower light from the same endpoint.
