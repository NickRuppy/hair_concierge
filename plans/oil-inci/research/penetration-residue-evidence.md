# Penetration and residue: evidence and candidate rules

Research date: **2026-10-01**. Status: **proposal for decision; not a signed operational standard**. Scope: `penetration_potential={penetration_potential,surface_led,unknown}` and `build_up_potential={low,elevated,unknown}` only. All URLs below were read or retrieved as indexed primary-paper text on this date. No products classified, calibration performed, or customer claims proposed.

## Recommended direction

Retain both properties with explicit unknowns, but distinguish their evidence strength. Ingredient penetration can be supported by direct experiments. Prediction of repeated finished-product accumulation from INCI alone is not scientifically established. Build-up can instead be a conservative **persistent-residue propensity judgment**, as already contemplated by the property proposal, provided its policy anchors are approved separately from the underlying science.

Do not operationalize either property as an exhaustive binary scientific fact. `surface_led` requires affirmative evidence for expected surface-dominant action; it is not the complement of an admitted penetrating-ingredient list. `low` requires positive evidence for limited persistent residue; it is not the absence of a flagged silicone.

## Primary evidence register

**P1 — Ruetsch et al., 2001, J. Cosmetic Science 52:169–184.** Cross-sectional TOF-SIMS detected coconut-oil characteristic ions within hair and contrasted this with mineral oil. This supports distinct behavior of the tested oils, not a universal molecular-size cutoff. Polarity/protein affinity was a proposed explanation, not a validated INCI scoring equation. The retrieved abstract did not establish donor count or enough protocol detail to generalize to every application time. TRI/Princeton and Marico affiliations mean the studies are not independent consumer efficacy trials. [Original paper PDF mirror](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=f6a1b75a588d6638e5790ea8829dfe109acc4023&repid=rep1&type=pdf) (indexed primary text available; direct PDF fetch failed).

**P2 — Rele and Mohile, 2003, J. Cosmetic Science 54:175–192.** Coconut, sunflower and mineral oil were compared using hair-damage/protein-loss endpoints; coconut performed differently from the other tested oils. Protein loss is an outcome, not a direct spatial assay. Failure to reduce that endpoint cannot establish zero penetration. No clinical subject count or universal effective concentration can be recovered from the abstract. [PubMed primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12715094/).

**P3 — Keis et al., 2005, J. Cosmetic Science 56:283–295.** Applied 0.2 cc oil to 2 g hair, massaged five minutes, and examined surface adhesion/optics over time and after heating. The changing signals for coconut, olive and sunflower were interpreted as possible penetration; these are indirect measurements. Mineral oil largely retained a stable surface film over 24 hours; the authors still allowed limited cuticular entry. The paper itself warns that surface optics cannot locate sunflower oil within the fibre. Thus it supports a mineral-oil surface-led anchor and time dependence, but not a sunflower nonpenetration rule or a universal overnight requirement. [Full primary paper](https://1.oliveoiltimes.com/library/olive-oil-hair.pdf).

**P4 — Gode et al., 2012, J. Cosmetic Science 63:27–31.** Radiolabelled coconut oil: 100 mg Indian hair soaked in 1.5 mL oil, sampled at one and six hours; surface measurements used two fibres and total measurements five, in triplicate sets. This is a small analytical experiment, not independent participant replication. Surface oil was distinguished from total oil by extraction/solubilization and subtraction; authors discuss method refinements and internal-consistency work still needed. It adds direct chemical evidence but supplies neither a minutes-to-effective-benefit threshold nor a justified percentage for a retail blend. [Author-uploaded primary PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Nitesh-Bhalla/publication/223969077_Quantitative_measurement_of_the_penetration_of_coconut_oil_into_human_hair_using_radiolabeled_coconut_oil/links/573530df08aea45ee83b1636/Quantitative-measurement-of-the-penetration-of-coconut-oil-into-human-hair-using-radiolabeled-coconut-oil.pdf), [bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22487449/).

**P5 — Marsh et al., 2024, International Journal of Cosmetic Science, DOI 10.1111/ics.12978.** Differential extraction supported penetration of coconut, *Camellia oleifera* and safflower oils. Isotope-labelled triolein/NanoSIMS localized material in the lipid-rich cell membrane complex. **Plant oils: 1 g oil/g hair, 24 h at 40°C; pure TAG comparison: 3 h at 75°C; extraction samples n=4.** These are laboratory exposures. Tested TAG penetration increased with shorter chains **and unsaturation**; “only saturated short-chain oils penetrate” is not sustainable. P&G/academic collaboration; extracted subsamples are not a clinical population. [Primary article and indexed full methods](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ics.12978). Direct opens sometimes returned the abstract; methods were available in the publisher's indexed text.

**P6 — Lourenço et al., 2024, Cosmetics 11(2):64.** Raman profiles were reported for coconut, avocado and argan in virgin/bleached Caucasian hair, with four tresses per treatment group. **1 g oil/g hair, 24 h at 25°C, followed by a 10% SLES wash**. Authors found internal oil-associated signals and different profiles by oil/damage. Mechanical results varied with humidity and damage. Penetration therefore cannot stand in for benefit. Raman assignment and surface-removal methodology deserve full-method review before these become equal-strength anchors to isotope localization. [Primary article](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9284/11/2/64) (publisher-indexed methods/results retrieved; direct page fetch failed).

**P7 — Berthiaume, Merrifield and Riccio, 1995, J. Society of Cosmetic Chemists 46:231–245.** GE Silicones researchers reported TOF-SIMS interior signals after treatment with several silicone emulsions, including high-viscosity dimethicone and amino-functional materials. Ion-beam etching addressed possible sectioning smear. They described the result as preliminary, with emulsion/amine/polymer characteristics possible contributors. This prevents a blanket “silicones cannot penetrate” rule; it does not prove any present-day silicone serum penetrates meaningfully. The penetration experiment's independent sample count was not established in this pass. [Primary paper mirror](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=b62521838342e9b97c411eaedccc13b20f26061d&repid=rep1&type=pdf), [readable primary-paper transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/523658841/Effects-of-Silicone-Pretreatment-on-Oxidative-Hair-Damage) (use article text, not the host-generated summary).

**R1 — Gruber et al., 2001, J. Cosmetic Science 52:131–136.** XRF assessed dimethicone on virgin brown hair from model shampoos. Adding polyquaternium-10 changed repeated deposition: build-up apparent without it was reduced and deposition more constant with it. Polymer molecular weight also mattered. This demonstrates formulation dependence, not a rule that PQ-10 always prevents build-up. Abstract does not supply a robust removal protocol or sample denominator. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11382849/).

**R2 — Ingredient for Hair Conditioning and Non-Buildup Effect, 2011.** EDS and sensory evaluation compared amodimethicone conditioner with/without Hydrolyzed Silk PG-Propyl Methylsilanediol Crosspolymer. The added ingredient suppressed observed accumulation. This is a direct counterexample to both “amodimethicone never accumulates” and “its presence fixes the product's accumulation behavior.” English abstract only reviewed; counts and complete conditions remain unverified. [Society of Cosmetic Chemists of Japan primary article](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sccj/45/2/45_122/_article/-char/en).

**R3 — Dow technical experiment, Silicone in Hair Care Overview, pp. 11–12.** A specified CE-8411 emulsion in shampoo/conditioner at 1% silicone active was measured by ICP-OES over repeated cycles in two hair-tress batches. Supplier reports noncumulative deposition under these conditions and approximately 90% removal after one clarifying wash. Protocol uses 0.8 or 0.4 g formulation/g hair. This supports possible removal and nonaccumulation despite deposition; it cannot establish easy removal for all silicones, shampoos or hair oils. Commercial technical evidence, not independent validation. [Supplier report](https://www.dow.com/documents/27/27-1/27-1967-01-silicone-in-hair-care-overview.pdf?iframe=true).

**R4 — Determination of the substantivity of emollients to human hair, 2007.** Three market shampoos and a model shampoo with 1.8% hydrogenated didecenes were studied through repeated use and subsequent SLES removal, using elemental/chemical analysis. The abstract establishes a relevant testing design but does not report enough numerical results for comparative low/elevated assignments. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17728945/).

**R5 — Dow raw-material specifications.** The selection guide distinguishes volatile Cyclopentasiloxane, Disiloxane and Trisiloxane from dimethicone grades spanning markedly different viscosities/molecular weights. The separate gum-blend documentation identifies high-viscosity Dimethiconol combined with carrier fluids. An evaporating carrier therefore does not establish an evaporating finished formula. These are supplier material properties, not hair accumulation tests. [Selection guide](https://www.dow.com/documents/27/27-1/27-1304-01-dow-hair-and-skin-care.pdf?iframe=true), [gum blends](https://www.dow.com/en-us/market/mkt-beauty-personal-care/sub-beauty-hair-care/dowsil-silicone-gum-blends.html).

## Operational subjudgments

Use named evidence judgments, not points or inferred percentages. Each retains source, exact INCI match, contradictory evidence and a short rationale.

| Subjudgment | Permitted conclusion | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ingredient_identity` | exact studied form / defensible material-family mapping / unresolved | Prevent coconut derivatives, botanical extracts or unspecified grades inheriting a parent's data. |
| `penetration_evidence` | direct chemical/spatial / indirect endpoint / affirmative surface evidence / absent or conflicting | Record observation and study conditions separately from label. |
| `formula_relevance` | sole conditioning lipid / wholly admitted lipid blend / mixed architecture / unresolved | An ingredient's presence establishes no effective concentration. First place is not a percentage. |
| `exposure_transfer` | study-like / materially different / unspecified | Captures timing, temperature, vehicle, dose and hair substrate as limitations; never creates consumer directions. |
| `residue_architecture` | all identified constituents evaporative / identified persistent film system / mixed or unresolved | Evaluate what remains after the carrier leaves, not just the first ingredient. |
| `removal_accumulation_evidence` | exact-formula repeated-use and removal data / related-formula data / absent | Persistence after one application is not accumulation over repeated cycles. |

No generic molecular-weight, fatty-acid-unsaturation, top-three, top-five or “before perfume” cutoff is justified by this pass. Molecular size, conformation and affinity are mechanistic contributors, not substitute measurements. Free fatty acids and whole triglycerides must not be conflated; skin absorption evidence is not hair-fibre evidence.

## Closed penetration candidates

Candidate decision tree, **engineering policy proposed for approval**:

1. Unresolved exact identity/formula: block the record upstream. Complete record but insufficient property evidence: `unknown` with the missing fact.
2. A pure exact oil in the admitted direct-evidence set: `penetration_potential`. Initially admit **Cocos Nucifera Oil**, **Camellia Oleifera Seed Oil**, **Carthamus Tinctorius Seed Oil** as candidates from P1/P4/P5. Coconut has the broadest method support; the others have narrower conditions. This means constituent potential, not dominant mass uptake, immediate action or finished efficacy.
3. A blend whose **entire identified conditioning-lipid system** comprises admitted oils: optional `penetration_potential`, **moderate confidence**, explicitly an untested blend extrapolation. If any conditioning lipid is unresolved, unadmitted or a competing architecture, this closure rule does not apply. No ratio is inferred. Reject an “any positive oil present” trigger.
4. Pure cosmetic mineral oil, or a system whose entire conditioning-lipid set is demonstrably equivalent to that studied mineral-oil material: `surface_led`, **moderate confidence**, supported by P1/P3. Material-grade differences and surface/cuticular overlap prevent a zero-penetration claim.
5. Mixed silicone/ester/alkane/plant architectures, unknown oil grades, supported but unadmitted oils, or indirect-only evidence: `unknown` unless an approved affirmative architecture rule or relevant finished-formula study resolves them. An incidental admitted oil does not decide the whole formula; absence from the admitted set is not a negative result.

**Neighbour rationale:** positive vs unknown turns on exact identity and whole-formula relevance; surface-led vs unknown turns on affirmative surface evidence. A formula can contain both mechanisms. The current property meaning does not establish a quantitative predominance threshold, and this proposal does not invent one.

**Confidence:** high requires convergent direct evidence plus unambiguous identity/formula transfer within the limited “potential” meaning. Moderate covers specific single-study anchors, material-family inference and admitted-blend extrapolation. If a consequential inference is too weak, output unknown rather than hiding it behind low confidence. Record confidence for an unknown as confidence in the insufficiency judgment, not probability of nonpenetration.

### Lexicon examples and exclusions

| Exact entry candidate | Evidence treatment | Explicit exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cocos Nucifera Oil | Direct positive candidate, P1/P4/P5; strongest initial anchor. | Coconut extract, water, alkanes, hydrogenated/fractionated derivatives and Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride do not inherit the whole-oil entry. |
| Camellia Oleifera Seed Oil; Carthamus Tinctorius Seed Oil | Direct positive candidates, P5, with laboratory exposure limitation. | Other Camellia species; sunflower is not safflower; cultivar/processing uncertainty must be retained. |
| Persea Gratissima Oil; Argania Spinosa Kernel Oil | Additional direct-study candidates, P6; retain pending full-method admission review. | No automatic outcome for benefits, damage fit or every related botanical form. |
| Olea Europaea Fruit Oil; Helianthus Annuus Seed Oil | P3 indirect support; unknown under initial strict closure. | Do not convert P2 protein-loss results into surface-led classification. |
| Paraffinum Liquidum / Mineral Oil | Affirmative surface-led candidate, P1/P3; verify material identity mapping. | Do not extend to every hydrocarbon, Squalane, Hydrogenated Polyisobutene or volatile alkane. |
| Dimethicone; Amodimethicone | Grade/formulation-dependent; P7 prevents a blanket nonpenetration class. | No universal “too large to enter” assertion; no universal elevated/low accumulation output. |
| Cyclopentasiloxane; Disiloxane; Trisiloxane | Volatile-material candidates, R5. | Volatility does not itself prove a surface-led conditioning role or low residue of a blend. |
| Dimethiconol | Persistent gum-system candidate when grade/system is established, R5. | Carrier evaporation does not remove the gum; presence alone does not quantify residue. |
| Jojoba oil, Castor oil, Squalane, Coco-Caprylate/Caprate | No admitted penetration or comparative build-up anchor in this bounded pass. | Unknown is a research gap, not evidence that these never penetrate or accumulate. |

## Build-up: candidate mechanism and choice

The scientific observation is a balance between deposition, loss/transfer and removal across a specified routine. INCI rarely reveals polymer grade, active delivery, dose, washing system or repeated deposition curve. The sources above do not validate a general ingredient score. Water insolubility establishes neither shampoo resistance nor accumulation; low sensory weight establishes neither volatility nor removability.

**Recommended conservative default:** keep `unknown` for ordinary mixed retail formulas until an approved propensity anchor applies. To operationalize the existing persistent-residue meaning without pretending to measure accumulation, consider this closed policy:

| Candidate | Output | Evidence/policy boundary and counter-signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete formula where every ingredient is established as evaporative under relevant use conditions and no residual conditioning solid/polymer/oil remains | `low` | Physical low-residue anchor. It is deliberately narrow. Unknown fragrance mixtures or nonvolatile constituents defeat closure; do not invent a negligible-tail threshold. |
| Entire residual conditioning system is an established durable nonvolatile silicone gum/film system, with exact grade or formulation evidence | `elevated` | **Provisional persistence-propensity policy**, not demonstrated accumulation or harm. Requires affirmative residual-film architecture, not any silicone match. Removal/plateau evidence for the exact formula can defeat this inference. |
| Exact-formula repeated-application/removal experiment demonstrates sustained rising retained load under its specified scenario | `elevated` under that documented scenario | Direct accumulation evidence; do not generalize to every cleanser/routine. A single positive residue measurement is insufficient. |
| Exact-formula experiment demonstrates a stable bounded residue/removal result | Potential `low`, only after comparator/meaning is agreed | A plateau can still be large and persistent. “Not increasing” alone does not establish low propensity under the proposed meaning. |
| Pure plant oil, ordinary mineral oil, unspecified Dimethicone grade, mixed persistent/evaporative formula, or silicone-free formula without an admitted anchor | `unknown` | Nonvolatility alone cannot rank relative accumulation. Do not grant plant oils a low default or mineral oil an elevated default. |

Do not numerically cancel a gum because a volatile carrier ranks earlier. Do not let PQ-10 or HPS automatically cancel another ingredient either: R1/R2 describe particular systems. The supplier removal result R3 is a counterexample to universality, not a “one shampoo removes everything” rule.

## Exact decisions for the owning session

1. **Penetration meaning:** recommend “credible constituent penetration relevant to the formula,” already compatible with the field name. If the desired meaning is instead predominantly internal action at recommended use, ingredient-only evidence will leave far more records unknown; do not silently choose between these meanings.
2. **Blend extrapolation:** approve or decline the all-admitted-conditioning-lipids rule. Recommendation: allow with moderate confidence and a visible extrapolation note; mixed architectures remain unknown. No top-N shortcut.
3. **Admission strength:** recommend initial P1/P4/P5 anchors; P6 avocado/argan admission should follow a full-method check. This is a finite methodological follow-up, not a request to begin product calibration.
4. **Surface architecture:** broader silicone/ester `surface_led` defaults require a separately documented architecture policy, because P7 and untested-material gaps prevent a general scientific negative. Recommend no blanket default.
5. **Build-up meaning:** recommend the bounded persistence-propensity interpretation with the very narrow low/elevated anchors above. Nick must approve any broader comparison scheme; if actual repeated accumulation is required, prefer unknown without exact-formula evidence.
6. **Low comparator:** no scientifically justified threshold/comparator emerged. Do not infer low from a stable plateau, penetrating oil, light feel, water solubility or silicone absence. Any wider low class is an engineering decision needing explicit rationale and later validation.

## Verification and limits

Checked source-to-rule consistency, exact allowed values, direct versus indirect endpoints, and noninferability of concentration. This is research-document verification; no application tests are relevant and none ran. Source access was uneven: P5 methods/P6 results were publisher-indexed text, P1/P7 used primary-paper mirrors/transcription, and R2/R4 were abstract-level. Full papers should be frozen and reviewed before lexicon admission where flagged. No systematic-review completeness claim is made.

The experiments are mostly ex-vivo fibres/tresses, often with commercial affiliations, high oil-to-hair loads and long contact times. Their independent donor counts are often unclear. Replicate fibres, extraction aliquots and tresses are not interchangeable with independent people. Finished-product delivery, ordinary brief exposure, repeated home routines and quantitative comparative residue thresholds remain unestablished.
