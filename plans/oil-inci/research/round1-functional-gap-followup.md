# Round 1 functional gaps: bounded evidence follow-up

Reviewed: 2026-10-01. Research only; this note proposes future dictionary enrichment. It does not amend the frozen round, its lexicon, labels, thresholds, or policy. No cohort, peer classifications, or finished-product outputs were consulted. Supplier trade names below identify raw materials only.

Generic role uncertainty is reducible for both previously unassessed ingredients. Persistence, retained dose, repeated-use accumulation, and perceived weight remain unresolved by INCI. “Film-forming capability” and “measured persistent film” must remain separate claims.

## Castor Isostearate Succinate

**Primary receipt.** [TC USA, Ingredients of Natural Origin brochure, 2020](https://www.canadiancosmeticcluster.com/uploads/3/7/9/8/37984461/tc_usa_ingredients_of_natural_origin_brochure__ver-2020_.pdf), PDF pages 6–7. Supplier-authored document hosted by Canadian Cosmetic Cluster; fetched and extracted directly. Page 6 identifies Zenigloss UP with the exact INCI and describes a castor-derived, hydrophobic liquid polymer with film-forming and gloss functions for hair and skin. Page 7 gives the same exact INCI for Zenigloss. Both are specified as viscous liquids; their listed molecular weights differ (6,900 versus 6,800 Da). These are raw-material specifications, not universal INCI constants.

Page 7 separately identifies Zenigloss UPH as **Castor Isostearate Succinate, Hydrogenated Castor Oil**, a paste. Its solid/paste behavior and melting range cannot be assigned to the single INCI. Page 6 likewise identifies the beeswax-containing derivative separately. No ingredient alias is created between these materials.

**Justified candidate additions:** castor-derived modified polymer; hydrophobic film-former; gloss agent. This is direct supplier identity/function evidence of useful strength for a generic role, but promotional and without a quantitative hair deposition/removal experiment in the reviewed pages. “Emollient” is plausible but not needed to capture the stronger explicit film-former evidence; leave that extra role unasserted on this receipt alone.

**Not justified:** intact castor fixed oil; penetration evidence transferred from castor oil; a silicone identity; UV-filter identity from an SPF-booster claim; wax/paste behavior transferred from UPH; exact molecular weight, raw-material grade, concentration, persistent-film category, accumulation or hair-weight prediction for an arbitrary formula. Hydrophobicity supports a possible surface-film mechanism, not quantified shampoo resistance.

**Access limit:** the [exact Zenigloss TDS](https://infinity-ingredients.co.uk/assets/uploads/product-downloads/Zenigloss_TDS_Rev-2_1410.pdf) returned HTTP 403. It was not used as evidence. The accessible supplier brochure resolves the generic function gap; blocked TDS access is not a blocker for that narrow conclusion.

## PPG-3 Caprylyl Ether

**Primary identity/function receipt.** [Kao Chemicals, KAO SOFCARE GP-1 material page](https://www.kaochemicals-eu.com/our-products/kao-sofcare-gp-1-ppg-3-caprylyl-ether) explicitly identifies PPG-3 Caprylyl Ether as an alkyl propoxylated ether and lists emollient, thickening and foam-related functions. This supports **ether emollient / conditioning auxiliary**, not ester, silicone, or intact plant oil. The page's lower oiliness, lower waxiness and low oily-residue comparison concerns skin sensory properties; it does not measure hair residue or repeated-use washout.

**Primary hair receipt.** [Kao GP-1 brochure, May 2019](https://www.kaochemicals-eu.com/uploads/docs/q/2/b/czl-br-sofcare-gp-1-en-00-1905.pdf?download=1), PDF page 2 (printed pages 2–3), gives the exact INCI, nonionic character and a silicone adsorption comparison after conditioning. Adding 1% GP-1 increased silicone adsorption in a specified behentrimonium chloride/fatty-alcohol conditioner. The measured endpoint is silicone per mass of hair, not retained GP-1. PDF page 3 also presents hair-surface images and supplier coating/conditioning claims. Sample sizes, uncertainty and repeated washout are not supplied in the displayed experiment.

**Justified candidate additions:** nonionic propoxylated ether; emollient; hair-conditioning auxiliary; possible formulation-dependent deposition modifier. Supplier evidence directly addresses hair interaction, so “unassessed generic function” can be narrowed. It does not justify calling the ingredient a persistent polymeric film-former or quantifying its own deposit. Low raw-material viscosity and lack of added VOC solvents do not establish evaporation from hair. Skin lightness, model-shampoo volume, and conditioner comparisons cannot establish weight or persistence in an arbitrary oil formula.

The [Kao technical data sheet](https://www.kaochemicals-eu.com/uploads/docs/8/3/i/dlt-tds-eng-v3-kao-sofcare-gp-1.pdf?download=1) is additional supplier documentation for identity, conditioning use and formulation compatibility, not a source of retail concentration or accumulation measurements.

## Polyquaternium-10: exact transfer ceiling

[Dow Hair Care Solutions Selection Guide](https://www.dow.com/content/dam/dcc/documents/27/27-2/27-2100-01-hair-cleansing-conditioning.pdf?iframe=true), PDF page 4, lists several UCARE grades with the **same Polyquaternium-10 INCI** under cationically substituted celluloses, with different supplier conditioning levels and recommended use ranges. PDF pages 7 and 10 attach particular deposition/styling benefits to named grades and contexts. These supplier rankings describe their materials/applications, not a validated persistent-film scale.

[Dow Personal Care Comprehensive Product Guide](https://www.dow.com/documents/27/27-2/27-2338-42-personal-care-comprehensive-product-guide.pdf?iframe=true), cationic-polymer table, gives JR-400 P and LR-400 the same INCI and the same 2% solution-viscosity range, but different nitrogen ranges (1.7–2.2% versus 0.8–1.1%). This is direct evidence that INCI does not encode a unique grade or substitution level. Avoid deriving molecular weight directly from these viscosity specifications, especially when comparing 1% and 2% solutions.

[Gruber et al., 2001, J Cosmetic Science 52:131–136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11382849/), primary study abstract reviewed: X-ray fluorescence measured dimethicone deposited on virgin brown hair after model-shampoo washes. Polyquaternium-10 altered deposition; higher polymer molecular weight increased silicone deposition in the tested systems, while polymer-containing shampoos showed less repeated-wash silicone buildup than the corresponding polymer-free systems. The authors were affiliated with Amerchol. This is direct experimental evidence for context/grade dependence, not an independent broad efficacy trial. It measures silicone, not Polyquaternium-10 mass. It establishes neither universal buildup nor universal absence of buildup from Polyquaternium-10.

**Ceiling:** retain generic cationic-cellulosic conditioning / film-forming capability and a context-dependent deposition mechanism. INCI alone cannot select a UCARE grade, its molecular weight/substitution, active concentration, coacervation behavior, retained mass, wash resistance or accumulation. It therefore cannot force an elevated persistence floor, quantify a film, or cancel other film signals. A generic possible surface contribution is defensible; a grade-specific persistence estimate is not.

## Reducible versus intrinsic uncertainty

| Question | What this follow-up resolves | What would still be needed |
|---|---|---|
| Generic function of the two gap ingredients | Exact supplier-INCI matches support film/gloss roles for Castor Isostearate Succinate and ether emollient/conditioning roles for PPG-3 Caprylyl Ether. | No further source needed for those narrow roles; dictionary adoption requires a later explicit version change. |
| Grade and formulation behavior | Polyquaternium-10 grade variation and formulation-dependent co-deposition are demonstrated. | Exact supplied grade, active amount, formula and use conditions; these are intrinsically absent from an ingredient name or rank. |
| Persistent film and accumulation | No universal estimate established. | Controlled deposition/removal and repeated-use hair measurements for the relevant formulation; ingredient hydrophobicity, skin sensory data and promotional “substantive” language cannot substitute. |

No reviewed source provides concentration from INCI rank, retail grade identity, user-specific heaviness, or a reusable numerical accumulation threshold. The remaining uncertainty is principally intrinsic to INCI; further generic supplier searching will not remove it.
