## 1. Per-product reasoning

### Slot 1 — alverde Leave-In Sprühkur Express 7in1 · `in_category`
Water plus a bulk sunflower-oil phase with no emulsifier and no solubiliser anywhere makes this a `two_phase` architecture on the v0.2 test, with the shake direction only corroborating. The tail marker (Levulinic Acid, r9) puts two persistent families above it — an unenumerated vegetable oil and a light ester/ether pair — with the one enumerated rich-band member, castor oil, sitting at r11 *below* the marker, so WT lands squarely on the new multi-family `moderate` row with its mandatory counter-signal. This product is also the exact case the v0.2 L9 tail-member rule was written for: hydrolyzed wheat protein is an L9 member but sits at r14 below the tail and carries no C1/C2 heat claim, so HEAT stays `not_claimed` and the binary stays `false` — the formula does not manufacture a claim. dm.de was treated as C2 because dm owns the alverde brand, which is what makes ROLE readable at all here. No focus route clears its anchor, so `general`.

### Slot 4 — alverde Nutri-Care 2-Phasen-Sprühkur · `in_category`
The clearest `two_phase` in the set: a rank-2 bulk soy oil with only a single alkyl polyglucoside at r10, which cannot carry that load. WT is the *single*-family row, not the multi-family one — soy and argan are both unenumerated liquid vegetable oils and therefore one family read medium-band by the §7.5 convention, and there is no enumerated rich-band member anywhere. G9 was checked explicitly: the `moderate` value rests on the observed rank of the oil load, never on the two-phase label. Three humectants high in the list (glycerin, sodium lactate, betaine) form a dominant humectant leg, which blocks any HUM upgrade independently of the absent claim. `detangling` was the closest focus call and failed both anchors — dm names detangling as one benefit among several rather than selling the product as a detangler, and the slip-dominant-light path needs WT `low`.

### Slot 11 — Balea Leichtkämmspray Pure Styling · `in_category`
An eleven-ingredient aqueous solution whose only conditioning species is Cetrimonium Chloride at r4, which lands it on the contradiction §21 says v0.2 knowingly did not repair: the COND `low` anchor speaks of a "short-chain quat" while §7.6 classifies Cetrimonium Chloride as a monomeric *long*-chain quat, and the SLIP `low` anchor is explicitly ruled out because a cationic species *is* above the tail. I took COND `low` / SLIP `moderate` and recorded the contradiction rather than smoothing it. PERS is `neutral_non_volatile` with the mandatory monomeric-quat note — the v0.2 repair working exactly as designed, since v0.1 would have forced this to `volatile_or_water_soluble` and under-warned on buildup. This is the only product where `detangling` qualifies, and it qualifies twice over: the C2 page sells it as a „Leichtkämmspray" that „erleichtert das Kämmen erheblich", and the architecture is independently slip-dominant and light.

### Slot 3 — Cantu Leave-In Repair Creme · `in_category`
A textbook LGN emulsion — Behentrimonium Methosulfate r5 with Cetearyl Alcohol r3 — carrying canola, shea butter and olive oil, so COND and WT both reach their top values through two independent clauses each, and the transfer caution attaches. The interesting outcome is at the other end: PQ-10 sits at r30, below the r28 marker, so the polymeric quat cannot set `permanent_cationic` and PERS falls back to `neutral_non_volatile` with the monomeric-quat note. HEAT is `not_claimed` with the binary `false` purely on authority — German retailer copy mentions heat protection, no C1/C2 Cantu source exists for the German market, and §2.4.1 rule 1 makes that a `claim_authority_gap` review route rather than a claim. ROLE is empty for the same authority reason: the captured German text reads like pack copy but carries only a C3 stamp.

### Slot 6 — Curlsmith Hydrate & Plump Leave-In · `in_category`
LGN pair plus castor oil and shea butter gives COND and WT their top values, and Guar HPTC r11 with PQ-10 r12 gives the first unambiguous `permanent_cationic` in the set — with the FS-20 check written into the record, because this is a silicone-free formula whose cationic polymers are among the most substantive materials in the category. SFR reaches `high` honestly: the polymer film observation is distinct from the lipid deposit already counted in COND. The decisive call was `curl_definition`: PVP at r33 is *above* the tail marker only because the marker sits at r36 behind a fourteen-member extract block, and it fails the separate "present as architecture" test, so a curl-brand product does not get a curl focus off a token polymer — which is what v0.2 change 23 is for. Primary is `smoothing`.

### Slot 5 — EVO Head Mistress Cuticle Sealer · `in_category`
A pre-neutralised Polyacrylamide / C13-14 Isoparaffin / Laureth-7 system — one of the enumerated non-LGN emulsion sub-types, which v0.1 had no row for. Phenoxyethanol at r6 is the §3.1.1 rule 3 early-marker case, so nothing below r6 could be established as architecture: that is what keeps Quaternium-80 (an enumerated silicone-functional quat) from setting `permanent_cationic`, and what keeps R2 at `none_visible`. `care_direction` is the cleanest application of v0.2 change 27 in the whole set — conditioning is led by Dimethicone and Dimethiconol with no R2 route and no material humectant or emollient leg above the marker, so the value is a deliberate `unknown`, not an interpolated `moisture`. HUM is a claim-free `formula_plausible` at `low` confidence that emits nothing user-facing, exactly as §7.9 intends.

### Slot 2 — ISANA PROFESSIONAL Leave-In Conditioner · `in_category`
LGN pair at r2/r7/r8 plus Guar HPTC at r5 gives COND `high`, SFR `high` and PERS `permanent_cationic`, all on above-tail observations. The distinctive move here is the §10.1 conflict tag: a formula-only WT `high` sits against the manufacturer's own C2 statement that the product cares „ohne das Haar zu beschweren", nothing resolves it, so the trace keeps `high` while the projected `weight_potential` is `moderate` and the field is marked uncertain — the rule's explicit purpose being not to encode unresolved uncertainty as a restrictive `high` that silently removes fine hair. rossmann.de was read as C2 on the private-label reading, which is what makes ROLE `[post_wash, ends_only]` rather than empty. `smoothing` is the only qualifying focus.

### Slot 12 — Kevin Murphy Young.Again Oil · `excluded_anhydrous`
Cyclopentasiloxane, Dimethicone, Dimethiconol and Bis-Cetearyl Amodimethicone lead the list and Water is declared at rank 23, so G0 excludes before any formula analysis. The name „Oil" did not decide it — the leading silicone architecture did, which is the §2.3 discipline. The §2.3.1 emission contract was applied literally: identity, `g0_state`, `out_of_category: true`, the formula record, and **no lean matching profile at all**. I emitted no §7 dimensions for this record because the contract makes them optional and because their only stated purpose is stress-case reuse; a lane that emits them will not be wrong, which is itself a comparability problem (ambiguity A22).

### Slot 7 — Maria Nila Curlicue Cream · `excluded_styling_first`
The HOLD anchor was run rather than the name. PVP is at rank 4, above the tail marker at r13, and the conditioning architecture behind it is genuinely thin: Cetyl Alcohol r5 has no cationic partner and so is not an LGN pair, there is no oil (a sunflower seed *extract*, not oil), no silicone, and the only quat is the structurally opaque Quaternium-95 at r11 — Polysorbate 20 and Carbomer are solubiliser and rheology materials. That is `meaningful_hold_route`, and the product's own directions and manufacturer positioning lead on texture and hold. I considered `provisional_boundary` and rejected it: the formula half of the exclusion anchor is independently sufficient, and the ambiguity in this record is about claim *authority* for the positioning half, not about the architecture. Emitted with no lean profile, plus three informational dimensions flagged non-authoritative.

### Slot 13 — Neqi Diamond Glass Ultimate Styling Spray · `in_category`
The only product in the set with both a heat and an anti-frizz claim verified at C2 on a German manufacturer page. It is not excluded despite „Styling" in its name: the HOLD anchor returned `incidental_film`, the silicone route sits above the fixative polymer, and the C2 positioning makes no hold claim — a blow-dry primer with meaningful conditioning is an explicit Include. HEAT is `claim_only` with the binary `true` and a mandatory review route, because the manufacturer's „heat-activated polymers" are not on the closed L9 list and adding them would need new peer-reviewed evidence and a version bump. HUM is capped at `claim_only` because the glycol leg was read conservatively as a dominant humectant architecture under the §7.9 default — the alternative solvent reading would give `formula_plausible`, so the reading is recorded, not hidden. `heat_styling` is primary because the claim and a verbatim „mit Hitze … föhnen" direction sentence both exist.

### Slot 10 — Olaplex N°.6 Bond Smoother · `in_category`
LGN pair plus a persistent silicone film plus an ester package gives COND `high`, SFR `high` and WT `high`, with a real counter-signal recorded against the last of those: Isohexadecane r4 and Isododecane r8 are volatile carriers that contribute nothing to residue. The two consequential calls are both about authority and rank. First, no German-language Olaplex manufacturer page could be located, so the ubiquitous „Hitzeschutz bis 232 °C" exists only at C4/C5 and `provides_heat_protection` is **false** with a `claim_authority_gap` review route — an authority outcome, not a formula verdict. Second, the silanetriol at r24 sits ten positions below the r14 marker, so R2 is `none_visible`; that single call keeps the product out of the §10.3 row-3b repair-film path and out of the `repair` focus, while the Bis-Aminopropyl Diglycol Dimaleate at r11 opens an R3 `chemistry_candidate` flag that R14 makes invisible to `damage_fit`.

### Slot 9 — Redken Extreme Anti-Snap · `in_category`
Phenoxyethanol at **rank 3** is the §3.1.1 rule 3 case verbatim, and it dominates this record: COND, WT, PERS, HUM and R2 were all held at the level the architecture supports, marked uncertain, and dropped a confidence step rather than mechanically collapsed. WT is the multi-family `moderate` row across an amino-silicone, an ester emollient and a fatty-alcohol/quat pair. PERS is `ph_dependent_cationic` on Amodimethicone r4, with Quaternium-33 recorded as `quat_structure: unresolved` and explicitly not promoted. HEAT is `claim_only` with the binary `true` on a verified C2 German-language claim and no L9 member — hydrolyzed *soy* protein is not the tested wheat protein and Quaternium-33 is not Quaternium-70. The focus is `general` even though the heat claim is real, because §10.2 requires a heat-tool direction sentence that the C2 page does not contain.

### Slot 8 — Schwarzkopf GLISS Sprüh-Conditioner Ultimate Repair · `in_category`
The hardest FORM call in the set: the direction opens „Vor Gebrauch schütteln!", which reads two-phase, but a silicone emulsifier (Cetyl PEG/PPG-10/1 Dimethicone r10) is declared, and v0.2 makes `two_phase` an architecture test with the shake direction demoted to corroboration — so `emulsion`, non-LGN, marked uncertain. Three persistent families above the tail with no rich-band member give the multi-family WT `moderate`; PQ-16 gives `permanent_cationic`, which combines with the `refresh` role to fire a §14 review trigger. R2 is the clean v0.2 change-17 case: Hydrolyzed Keratin at r6, high in the list, is still `none_visible` because rank is not what makes a protein an R2 route. HEAT is `claim_only` / binary `true` on a C2 „Hitzeschutz bis 230 °C" claim with no L9 member — with the caveat that the verbatim claim was retrieved from a sibling-product page on the same manufacturer domain.

## 2. The hardest calls

1. **EVO SFR `moderate` vs `high` (slot 5).** The `high` anchor needs a continuous film route *plus* a lubrication route; the Dimethicone/Dimethiconol pair is one M1 mechanism, and the only candidate second route (macadamia oil, isoparaffin) sits below a rank-6 marker. I took `moderate` on G3 grounds. This single call flips `focus.primary` between `general` and `smoothing` for a product literally named a Cuticle Sealer. Same reasoning pattern applied to Neqi; the opposite outcome was reached for Olaplex and Gliss, where the second route is itself above the tail.
2. **Olaplex `provides_heat_protection = false` (slot 10).** Faithful to §2.4.1 rule 1 and §13.3 rule 1, and it will look wrong to anyone who has read the German pack. The rule is doing what R12 designed it to do — the review route exists precisely so a human decides whether the manufacturer source was simply not found — but it means the production binary for one of the best-known heat-claiming leave-ins in the German market turns on whether a search located `olaplex.com/de-de`.
3. **Neqi `curl_definition` vs `heat_styling` primary (slot 13).** The `curl_definition` anchor is formally satisfied by any above-tail fixative polymer with compatible COND/WT and requires no curl evidence, and the §10.2 rank order then puts it above `heat_styling`. I rejected it on the direction sentence („mit Hitze und auf Spannung föhnen" is the opposite instruction), which is a judgment the rule text does not authorise. See A19.
4. **Gliss `emulsion` vs `two_phase` (slot 8).** A declared emulsifier against an explicit shake instruction. Architecture won per change 5. Had `two_phase` won, DOSE would move to `high`, the shake caution would be emitted from FORM, and the record would gain the two-phase review trigger.
5. **Maria Nila `excluded_styling_first` vs `provisional_boundary` (slot 7).** Excluding removes the lean profile entirely; a `provisional_boundary` would keep the product in-category with everything uncertain. The formula read is unambiguous; the doubt is about whether C5 positioning may carry the anchor's second half (A15).
6. **Redken R2 `unknown` vs `candidate` (slot 9).** A qualifying silane derivative exists at r14 under a rank-3 marker. `unknown` is the conservative call and keeps the product out of the highly-damaged tier — arguably the wrong product answer for a leave-in sold for breaking hair, and exactly the consequence R14's row 3b was added to relieve.
7. **ISANA trace WT `high` with projected `moderate` (slot 2).** Correct under §10.1, but it means the record carries a `high`-derived DOSE caution alongside a `moderate` weight prior (A14).
8. **Balea COND `low` with SLIP `moderate` (slot 11).** Knowingly unrepaired per §21; I reproduced rather than resolved it, so round 2 can measure whether it still bites.
9. **Neqi PERS `neutral_non_volatile` vs `permanent_cationic` (slot 13).** Silicone Quaternium-18 is two positions below the marker. `permanent_cationic` would change the projection to `high` and emit the buildup caution.
10. **Slot 1 / slot 4 DOSE `high`.** Driven by the two-phase trigger while WT is `moderate` (A7).
