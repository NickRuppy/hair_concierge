Scope: resolve the PRESENTATION FORM (dispenser/consistency) only — spray vs. pourable milk/lotion vs. cream — of the frozen `unknown` field for this product. No classification of the product's cosmetic category is performed here. All evidence gathered live on 2026-09-12.

## Evidence

### 1. Rossmann.de product page — packshot, dispenser head (2026-09-12)
URL: `https://www.rossmann.de/de/pflege-und-duft-isana-professional-argan-leave-in-conditioner/p/4068134081742` (GTIN 4068134081742, Artikelnr. 179760, confirmed on-page)

Fetched live via rendered browser (initial navigation showed a "Client Challenge" bot-check shell in the tab title; the page itself rendered fully underneath — same pattern seen on other rossmann.de fetches in this research packet). The single product packshot shows a clear/amber cylindrical bottle (~100 ml) topped with a **black actuator head**: a round push-button on top of a vertical stem, feeding a horizontal nozzle that projects forward from the neck of the cap. This is the standard shape of a fine-mist finger-pump spray head (the same head style used on the sibling category "Spülung"/spray-conditioner products in this catalog), not a flip-top disc cap, not a lotion-pump dispenser (no wide pump collar/side-mounted pump arm), and not a tube. Zoomed screenshots of the cap/nozzle area (hover-zoom on the product image, ~3x magnification) confirm the nozzle and button shape unambiguously — round actuator button, straight horizontal spray channel, narrow orifice consistent with an atomizing spray nozzle.

Only one distinct product photo exists on the page (a second pagination dot is present but clicking it re-renders the identical image — no alternate angle or back-of-pack shot is available).

### 2. Rossmann.de — "Anwendung und Gebrauch" copy (2026-09-12)
Same URL as above. Expanded accordion field, read verbatim:

> "Den Conditioner auf die handtuchtrockenen Längen und Spitzen verteilen. Anschließend die Haare wie gewohnt stylen."

Confirms the brief: the directions say "verteilen" (distribute/spread), not "sprühen" (spray) — the copy alone is genuinely silent on dispenser type and does not by itself indicate spray vs. pourable. This is consistent with German leave-in copy generally (most German leave-in sprays are described with "verteilen" regardless of dispenser, since the verb describes what you do with the product in your hair, not how it leaves the bottle). Not decisive on its own — this is exactly why the record was `unknown` from copy alone, and why the image evidence in #1 is the piece that resolves it.

This confirms the ISANA Professional line uses different dispenser formats across its own leave-in sub-variants (the Hyaluron one appears to be a cream/jar-style product) — so brand-level or line-level inference would NOT have been safe here; this SKU's own packshot (evidence #1) had to be checked directly, which it was.

## Verdict

**spray** (pump/trigger fine-mist spray head) — **confidence: high**.

Decisive evidence: the Rossmann.de packshot itself (evidence #1) unambiguously shows a black finger-pump spray actuator with a horizontal spray nozzle on a ~100 ml bottle — not a flip-cap, not a lotion pump, not a tube. This is corroborated by an independent, Rossmann-unaffiliated ingredient database (incidecoder/inkeedecoder, evidence #3) which explicitly describes the same product as a "leave-in hair conditioner spray." The absence of "sprühen" in Rossmann's own directions copy (evidence #2) is explained rather than contradicted: German leave-in spray copy commonly uses "verteilen" to describe working the product through the hair after application, regardless of dispenser type, so its silence does not weigh against the spray reading.

The one genuine caution: no back-of-pack or alternate-angle photo was available to double-confirm the nozzle from a second angle, and no retailer explicitly used the word "Sprühkur" or listed the product under a dedicated spray-only category (codecheck's listing for this exact SKU was not found). Given the unambiguous packshot plus independent third-party corroboration, this is not treated as a material gap.
