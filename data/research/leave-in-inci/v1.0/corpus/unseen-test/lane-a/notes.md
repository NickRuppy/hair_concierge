## Where the standard was ambiguous for this evidence, and what I chose

1. **`tail_marker` vacuity (§3.1.1 clause 6, T17 H1) — the clause states two non-equivalent tests.** The *operational* instruction says: "read every ingredient at or below the marker's rank and confirm none of them is anything other than a capped material, a fragrance/allergen declaration or a colourant." The *intent-level* formulation two sentences later says a non-vacuous marker's tail contains "at least one ingredient the architecture read would genuinely have placed there (a real conditioning polymer, oil, or comparable species)". They diverge for pH adjusters and preservative boosters, which are neither capped-list materials nor real architecture. **u4** has SODIUM HYDROXIDE and CAPRYLHYDROXAMIC ACID below its marker; **u5** has CITRIC ACID. **I applied the deterministic operational test** (⇒ neither marker vacuous), because clause 6 explicitly tells the reader to "check it the same deterministic way clause 5 checks plausibility", and because the intent-level reading would make nearly every well-ordered INCI list vacuous — swallowing the rule rather than narrowing it, contrary to the clause's own "this is narrower than it may look". **Neither reading changes a single value** on u4 or u5: nothing credited as architecture on those records is credited on rank alone (the LGN pairs and emollients are family-coherent at r3–r9). I recorded the test result and its reasoning in each record's `tail_marker.vacuity_test`. This is the item most likely to produce a lane-vs-lane difference in *trace* rather than in *values*.
2. **§2.3.2 clause 1 vs clause 2 on u3.** Documented at length in u3 §3–4 above. Both readings are fully specified in the record so a reviewer can flip it without re-deriving.
3. **Rule 7(a)'s tolerance on u6.** "Set-for-set … order noise aside" does not say how many appended fragrance chemicals are tolerable. I read three additional declared ingredients as a set difference, not order noise, and took the conservative branch (rule 3 stands). The full consequence of the opposite reading is written into u6's `review_routing.notes` — HEAT `claim_only`, binary `true`, `focus.primary` → `heat_styling`.
4. **LAYER (§8.4) has no defined trigger condition anywhere in the standard.** §8.6 says LAYER "fire[s] when true" and §8.4 gives only the German string and the prohibition on computing a compatibility matrix. No section states *when* layering risk is true. **I fired LAYER on no record**, because choosing a trigger (e.g. "a substantive cationic architecture is present") would be inventing an untraced rule, and it would fire on four of the six products here. A lane that invents a trigger will diverge from this one on u1, u4, u5 and u6.
5. **SHN (§8.1) has no present/absent anchor.** §8.1 says only "Emit `smoothing_shine_qualifier: present | absent` as a qualifier on the smoothing route", and M3 is defined as "shine as the optical consequence of alignment", fed by M1/M2. **My reading: SHN is `present` when `smoothing_route` is a continuous FILM type (`silicone_film`, `cationic_alignment`, `fixative_film`) and `absent` when it is `emollient` or `none`.** This fires on u1 and u6 and not on u4/u5, and it keeps §8.6's "an empty `fired` array is the normal case" true. The alternative (any route ≠ `none` ⇒ present) would fire on all four in-category records.
6. **EXPO `aromatic_or_allergen_exposure` has no §18 string.** §18 defines strings for `no_listed_fragrance_signal` and `fragrance_declared` only, while L8's enum has four values. For u1, u5 and u6 I emitted „Enthält deklarierte Duftstoffe." — factually true of the stronger flag, the only defined string in the family, and understating rather than overstating (the conservative direction). A lane that emits nothing for this value will differ on three records' `cautions_de`.
7. **The buildup string on §8.5's new WT limb.** §18's row is keyed on "PERS → `high` + buildup caution", but T17 H6 now also fires the caution when `weight_potential` is `high` with a persistent family, at `persistence: moderate` — and §21.3's own ledger says the affected gold-set slots reopen "`hinweise` record **and** `profile.cautions_de` … for the added buildup entry". So a string is clearly intended. I emitted the one defined buildup string on u4, u5 and u6, noting that its „Bleibt lange im Haar" opener is keyed on a PERS `high` this record does not have. §18 needs a WT-limb variant.
8. **`smoothing_route` has no `unknown` member** (closed five-value set), which is awkward on u1, whose captures would type it differently. Since it is trace-only and never projected (G14), I typed it off the record's own frozen `raw_inci`/fingerprint and recorded the alternative type explicitly, rather than resolving the conflict by preference — the consuming two-observation test passes under both readings, so the focus does not move.
9. **Which §7 dimensions an excluded record may carry (§2.3.1, open gap §17.24).** They are "optional" with no stated subset. I emitted **none** on u2 and u3, keeping only identity, G0 + rationale, `out_of_category`, the formula/tail-marker reading and the architecture read that *is* the G0 rationale's own evidence. A lane that emits informational dimensions will produce a different exclusion-record shape (which is precisely what §17.24 predicts).
10. **§2.4's "blocks the whole analysis only when the dominant architecture cannot be resolved" (u1).** The standard does not say what "blocks" means operationally — the record still has a decidable G0 and several conflict-independent dimensions. I read it as: emit every value the conflict does not decide, emit `unknown` for every value it does, and route the record to review. That is G5's "lower the **smallest** affected scope; do not blank the record" applied to a conflict that happens to reach four dimensions.
