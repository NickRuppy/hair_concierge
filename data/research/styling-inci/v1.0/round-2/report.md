## 2. Owner questions (ranked by impact)

### Q1. Which words make a curl product „care + curl definition" (then it is hand-reviewed)? (RG2-1)
Rule R1-Q3 sends a product that promises both care and curl definition to manual review. Lanes disagree on what counts as each half:
- **Care half.** Option (a): only a care job the product promises (spendet Feuchtigkeit, pflegt, repariert, entwirrt — also „Pflege", „Care", „Detangling" in the name). Option (b): any care word, including an adjective on the styled result („gepflegte Locken").
- **Definition half.** Does reviving or reactivating curls count as curl definition?

Examples:
- **Redken Refreshing Curl Mist** („revitalisiert und reaktiviert Locken … für gestylte und gepflegte Locken").
  - Lane A, reading (a): a Styling refresher, held until the Leave-in addendum.
  - Lane B, reading (b): mixed, manual review.
- **Wella EIMI Nutricurls Fresh Up** („Curl Refreshing Detangling Spray", „Locken reaktivieren").
  - Lane A: „Detangling" is care and reactivation counts as definition, so mixed.
  - Lane B: Leave-in, because the conditioner PQ-10 is real care.
- The same question sits behind Balea Sea Salt Care & Define (promise class only) and the sealed slot 7, Moroccanoil Glättende Creme (both lanes: mixed).

**Recommendation.** Choose (a), and say that curl revival is not definition: the definition half needs „definiert/Definition". This follows the false-signal rule SFS-15, keeps „mixed" for products that really sell both jobs, and stops every curl spray with a care adjective from going to manual review.
- Under (a), Redken Curl Mist becomes a held refresher and Nutricurls Fresh Up becomes Leave-in. Nutricurls also needs a tier re-stamp first (P2-6): its promise lines come from a retailer, so it may stop at the evidence gate instead.

### Q2. A water-based anti-frizz „Serum": Oil or Leave-in? (RG2-2)
Balea Haarserum Anti-Frizz Glatt & Glossy has water first, Cetearyl Alcohol at rank 3, and its conditioner (Behentrimonium Chloride, rank 12) just one rank below the preservative.
- The v0.2 serum test counts the conditioner only above the preservative. So the letter says **Oil** (lane A).
- The registry names the product **Leave-in** (lane B). The registry may not overrule this step, so v0.2 contradicts itself.
- For contrast, Balea Pro Haarserum Glow & Shine has its conditioner at rank 5 and is Leave-in under both readings.

**Recommendation.** Leave-in. Let the serum test read the conditioner the way the D6 rule already does: down to the first pH adjuster after the preservative. The registry outcome then follows from the rules, with no override.

### Q3. Should the blind S0 packet keep the words that name a product's job? (RG2-3, P2-1)
The blind packet removes finish words (Glanz, Shine, Gloss, Finish) everywhere, including the product name. For shine products those words *are* the job.
- **OSiS Sparkler**: „ein Glanz-Spray für … glänzendes Haar … leichtes Finish" became „ein [CLAIM]-Spray für … [CLAIM] Haar … leichtes [CLAIM]".
  - Lane B stopped it at the evidence gate.
  - Lane A guessed a shine spray.
- **taft x Gliss „Glanz-Spray … Silky Shine"** became „[CLAIM]-Spray … Silky [CLAIM]".
  - Lane B read the next line („Styling & Pflege in einem") and called it mixed.
  - Lane A reconstructed „Shine" and called it Styling.

In this round that cost 3 route splits and 2 primary-job splits. A lane that reconstructs blanked words is no longer blind, so the redaction also weakens blinding.

**Recommendation.** Yes:
- keep nouns that name the job (Glanz-Spray, Shine, Gloss, Finish/Finishing, Haarspray, Wachs, Puder) in the S0 lines and the name;
- keep removing hold-strength, flexibility and finish *grading* adjectives, numbers and scales;
- put the unredacted product name into the claims packet.

### Q4. Extend the NEQI Diamond Glass Ultimate ruling to its „All" sibling? (RG2-25)
NEQI Diamond Glass All has the same silicone film as the Ultimate, without the fixative. The Ultimate is ruled Leave-in.
- By the rules alone, the All is „Styling + Hitzeschutz in einem", so mixed and manually reviewed (lane A).
- Lane B called it Leave-in.

**Recommendation.** Yes, extend the registry row. Leave-in is also what the rules give once the mixed reading is set aside. Do it after the possibly-truncated INCI is checked (P2-3).

## 6. Normalisation used for comparison

Same 21 core fields and normalisation as round 1, with these changes:
- **Reason codes (R2).** Lane A now writes `reason_code` only for boundary routes, so its pair rule is read from its `rule_path`. Lane B uses a new code vocabulary, mapped as listed in `diff.json` → `normalisation`. B's `dry_hair_finish` without a named pair rule stays unmapped.
- **Informational values (R2).** Lane A emits formula values on 43 non-Styling records, marked `informational_and_non_authoritative`. Standard §4.7 makes these optional, and the sealed file does not score them. They are masked before comparison.
- **application_moment.** Both lanes now emit {primary, also}. The primary is compared; `also[]` is a supplementary field.
- **New supplementary fields.** `ingredient_flags` and `application_moment_also`. Supplementary fields are not in the overall percentage.
- **Unseen scoring (R2).** A soft value matches the expected value or any listed alternative (sealed-file convention); exact-value matches are reported separately. UNDETERMINED values are not scored and are reported as rule gaps.
- **Round-1 comparison.** Uses `round-1/diff.json` (all 107 round-1 records are carried under the same slug).
- **S0 redaction check.** Computed from `r2/cohort.json` (raw vs `s0_redacted_*`). The method is in `diff.json` → `s0_redaction_check.summary`.
