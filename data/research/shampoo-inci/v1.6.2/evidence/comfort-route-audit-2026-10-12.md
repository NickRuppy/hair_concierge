# Audit of the E4 comfort list — rinse-off evidence (2026-10-12)

Cited research check (hair-care-expert subagent, Sonnet, read-only), asked by Nick as a devil's-advocate test of a possible double standard: is the current comfort list itself proven from a rinse-off shampoo? Limits: PubMed/PMC pages were partly blocked; work relied on Europe PMC abstracts and secondary pages; items marked unverified could not be opened.

Bottom line: no independent, peer-reviewed trial was found in which any of the eight listed ingredients, as a lone active in a rinse-off shampoo, soothed an irritated or itchy scalp. Decent evidence is leave-on, on skin rather than scalp, or multi-active. The list is "plausible leave-on evidence plus mechanism plus safety clearance" — a defensible cosmetic-formulation heuristic, not a proven rule. The gap to the rejected candidates is small; the listed ingredients are better characterised on safety, and panthenol and colloidal oat have real leave-on trials.

|---|---|---|---|

Product implications adopted (ruling R18): keep the formula requirement as a plausibility filter, never present it as proof; avoid "contains soothing X" user copy; persistent symptoms → dermatologist.
