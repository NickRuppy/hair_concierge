# What makes a shampoo suit a sensitive scalp — devil's-advocate check (2026-10-12)

Cited research check (hair-care-expert subagent, Sonnet, read-only) arguing for a more generous rule. Limits: little primary literature retrievable; several facts from abstracts or search summaries are marked unverified in the source report. Nothing here is high-grade evidence.

1. Steelman of the rejected candidates: aloe (small SLS-patch leave-on study, weak — https://search.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/pt/wpr-99993 ; counter: radiation-dermatitis review https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/aloe-vera-for-preventing-radiation-induced-skin-reactions-a-syste/); Lactococcus lysate (best match: placebo-controlled 4-week shampoo study, 3 %, 20 per arm, sponsor-run, results tables not readable — https://coms.events/IFSCC2022/data/abstracts/en/abstract_0270.html ; Sante's position 7–9 suggests a lower dose); arginine (NMF, mechanistic — https://assets.bmctoday.net/practicaldermatology/pdfs/pd0712_ftr_nmfreview.pdf); lauryl lactate (nothing usable). The same rinse-off gap holds for panthenol, allantoin and bisabolol.
2. Levers: "sensitive scalp" is a self-reported symptom syndrome; shampoo is a named trigger (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3500070/); management evidence is low (case series, expert opinion — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10697758). Consensus advice: mild amphoteric or soft anionic surfactants, no fragrance. Surfactant system is the most plausible driver: SLES alone is the irritation baseline, amphoterics lower it (https://library.scconline.org/v049n03/51); sulfosuccinates soften harsher surfactants (class review, https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jos/55/9/55_9_429/_article); amino-acid surfactants well tolerated in a small patch study (unverified). Caveats: glucosides and CAPB carry allergy reports; "sulfate-free" is not automatically gentler. pH is a weak discriminator for shampoos. Fragrance-free is a reasonable precaution, not proven (rinse-off sensitisation model — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30422886/). Menthol: neutral-to-negative flag (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6301073).
3. Proposed alternative rules: (A) mild surfactant system — no alkyl sulfate / alkyl ether sulfate as a main surfactant; (B) fragrance-free. Applied to the four failing Track A products: A passes Sebamed Every-Day only (Sante's first surfactant is Sodium Coco-Sulfate; both ISANAs are SLES + CAPB); B passes none (all four declare Parfum; verified on the full Sante INCI).

Honest counter-argument recorded: A and B rest on consensus and mechanism, not controlled shampoo trials; a mild-base product can still contain irritants (Parfum, propylene glycol).

Nick's ruling R18 (2026-10-12): claims alone never pass ("we don't just trust marketing claims"); option C — the formula gate passes on a listed comfort route OR a mild surfactant base. Main-session precision: "mild base" = no prominent strong anionic of either C3 type, so olefin/alkylbenzene sulfonate chassis do not pass either.
