# Conditioner focus selection decision guide

Status: pilot recalibration guidance
Scope: research-only rinse-out Conditioner profiles
Decision: retain `detangling`, `smoothing`, `shine`, and `repair` as distinct endpoints; tighten when any may become a primary focus

## v1.6 care-direction and repair-support boundary

`care_direction` and `repair_support_level` complement the focus hierarchy; they do not replace it. `care_direction` is a product-formula direction, never an assertion that a user has a protein or moisture deficiency. Classify `protein` only for a material identifiable protein/peptide/keratin temporary-film route, `moisture` for coherent conditioning/humectant/emollient support without a dominant protein-film route, and `balanced` only for a substantive mixed protein-plus-moisture architecture. A neutral conventional conditioner remains `moisture`; `balanced` must not hide unresolved evidence. Formula-only calls remain E2.

`repair_support_level` separates ordinary conditioning from stronger temporary support: `low` is conventional conditioning/lubrication; `medium` requires a distinct temporary protein/peptide/keratin fibre-film route; `high` requires a materially stronger named bond route visible in the reviewed formula. This does not claim structural repair. Finished-product positioning without that route, generic silicone, oil, panthenol, ceramide, cationic polymer, generic repair naming, or ordinary conditioning remains `low`.

## Damage Fit boundary

Damage Fit is a broad product prior, not a claim that the product repairs hair. Map low conditioning to exactly `healthy`; map moderate conditioning and general high conditioning without a qualifying specialist route to exactly `healthy` plus `moderately_damaged`. Map high conditioning with a distinct protein/peptide/keratin fibre-film route, named bond chemistry, exceptional corroborated protection, or a relevant exact-product test to exactly `moderately_damaged` plus `highly_damaged`. The specialist result replaces the general-high set; never emit all three values. Generic silicone, oil, panthenol, ceramide, cationic polymer, repair naming, and generic lubrication candidates remain ordinary conditioning/smoothing support and do not qualify.

This rule recalibrates the pilot to eight `healthy` + `moderately_damaged` profiles and three `moderately_damaged` + `highly_damaged` profiles: NEQI through oat peptide, OGX through hydrolyzed collagen, and Balea Bond+ through its named bond pair. No pilot formula maps to low conditioning. Formula-only cases remain E2; a fresh v1.6 nine-property blind rerun is required before claiming repeatability.

## Research output contract

Every future eligible Conditioner researched for the database must receive exactly one `primary_focus` and zero to two `secondary_focus` values. This hierarchy is the canonical comparison output for the current research program: it forces the researcher to distinguish similar products and makes unresolved prioritisation visible to human reviewers.

The detailed direct properties and supported capabilities must also be preserved underneath the hierarchy. They prevent evidence loss and may later support a flat production matching model, but a flat capability list does not replace `primary_focus` and `secondary_focus` during this research phase. `primary_focus` means the most distinctive supported product purpose, not a measured promise that this effect is stronger in use than every secondary effect.

## Why the labels overlap

A conventional Conditioner base usually combines a cationic surfactant or protonatable amidoamine with fatty alcohols. Its main immediate effect is deposition and lubrication of the fibre surface. That one system can reduce wet-combing force, lower friction, improve surface alignment, reduce frizz, and increase reflected light. Therefore an INCI list can support several capabilities without proving that the product has several independent technologies.

The labels still answer different user questions:

- `detangling`: wet-use slip and reduced combing resistance;
- `smoothing`: dry surface alignment, lower friction, softer feel, and frizz control;
- `shine`: an optical finish endpoint;
- `repair`: damage-targeted temporary film, protection, or bond-specific support, never structural regeneration from a formula list alone.

They should not be fused in the research trace. Wet combing, dry friction/frizz, and optical shine are different measurable endpoints. The correction is to stop treating each supported endpoint as a separate ingredient technology and to make the primary-focus threshold more demanding.

## What the INCI can and cannot decide

### Formula-observable support

| Focus candidate | Typical supporting architecture | What does not establish it by itself |
|---|---|---|
| `detangling` | coherent cationic/fatty-alcohol base; stronger long-chain cationic route; compatible polymer deposition; high wet-slip potential | the fact that the product is a Conditioner |
| `smoothing` | strong dry-comb/surface-lubrication route beyond the base, such as silicone, substantive polymer, or substantial lipid/emollient support; exact frizz/smooth positioning may corroborate | one oil, one silicone, or a smooth-feel claim alone |
| `shine` | a distinct optical/alignment route or exact-product shine evidence | moderate shine expected from the same film already counted as smoothing |
| `repair` | distinct R5 protein/peptide film-support or R7 bond-review route, or exact-product damage endpoint evidence; damage/repair positioning may corroborate | silicone alone, panthenol, oils, ceramides, cationic polymers, biotin, or the word “repair” |

Cationic structure, concentration, counterion, hair damage, formula ratios, processing, and the finished lamellar/emulsion structure all affect deposition and combing performance. The consumer INCI omits most of that information. EU labelling also permits ingredients below 1% to appear in any order. Consequently the list supports a directional potential, not the deposited amount, sensory weight, persistence, buildup, or exact finished-product result.

## Deterministic hierarchy

Apply the following order after direct properties have been completed:

1. Exclude baseline conditioning. A conventional cationic base supports conditioning and detangling capability, but does not automatically make `detangling` the product's main purpose.
2. Identify distinct routes. Group all evidence that shares `M1_DEPOSITION_SURFACE_LUBRICATION`; do not count smoothing, detangling, and shine as three technologies merely because all three endpoints are plausible.
3. Test special-purpose eligibility first:
   - `repair` needs R5/R7 or exact-product endpoint evidence;
   - `color_care` needs a formula candidate plus exact-product positioning or product evidence;
   - `curl_support` needs high slip plus compatible weight/body architecture;
   - `lightness` needs low weight plus likely-preserving body.
4. Choose `smoothing` when dry surface control is the clearest practical differentiator and is supported beyond the ordinary base.
5. Choose `detangling` only when slip/combability is itself distinctive: it is clearly stronger than other routes, supported by a comparatively simple/light architecture or exact tangling/combability positioning, and no richer special-purpose route wins.
6. Choose `general` when the formula is a capable conventional Conditioner but no specific purpose clears the differentiator threshold. Supported capabilities still remain available in the detailed trace and future flat `functionalBenefits[]`.
7. Add at most two secondary foci. A secondary may represent a distinct user endpoint even when it shares part of a mechanism, but it must add useful matching information. Do not add `shine` when it is merely the optical consequence of the smoothing film.
8. Mark the primary uncertain when formula evidence cannot resolve two plausible product purposes. Do not use current catalog labels to break the tie.

## Repair versus smoothing

`smoothing` is the default interpretation of a surface-depositing lubrication architecture. Silicones, cationic polymers, lipids, oils, esters, and ceramides may all contribute to that route. They can temporarily improve feel, combability, frizz, or appearance without reversing fibre damage.

`repair` becomes eligible only when there is a damage-targeted route that is separable from ordinary lubrication. A credible protein/peptide film route may support temporary cosmetic repair; a substantiated bond route may support a bond-specific candidate. Formula-only evidence remains capped at E2 and must never be worded as structural restoration. If the only evidence is the same film that produces smoothing, select `smoothing` and retain any repair claim only as claim context.

## Detangling versus smoothing

The ingredient families are often similar because both begin with surface deposition and lower friction. The difference is the endpoint and the architecture around the base:

- a simple/light cationic base with standout wet-slip support can justify `detangling`;
- a richer silicone/polymer/lipid architecture with stronger dry-comb, softness, surface, or frizz support favors `smoothing`;
- when neither is distinctive beyond ordinary conditioning, use `general` rather than forcing `detangling` to win.

Operationally, two other rules should remain automatic rather than require repeated product-level approval: ingredients below the hidden 1% boundary remain weak rank evidence, and `general` is preferable to inventing a distinctive focus from an ordinary Conditioner base. Exact usage and application directions remain protocol metadata and are not comparison properties.

### Required focus explanation

For every future product, the primary and secondary focus record must list the exact INCI ingredients or formula pattern carrying each candidate route, the direct-property comparison that selected the winner, any exact-product positioning used only as corroboration, and the evidence ceiling. “Smoothing fits this product” or another suitability-only sentence is not an acceptable rationale. The explanation must also identify why a plausible competing focus lost and why a secondary adds a distinct comparison endpoint instead of double-counting the primary film route.

### Critical pilot checkpoint

The highest-value human review is therefore:

4. NEQI: review `weight_potential: moderate` as the conservative lean fallback while the detailed trace preserves higher deposition potential and unresolved uncertainty.

## Weight potential and the NEQI limit case

`weight_potential` is an ingredient-informed matching prior, not a prediction that a product will visibly flatten hair. It should use formula architecture rather than a count of “heavy” ingredients:

- `low`: compact/light architecture with no substantial additional deposition route;
- `moderate`: conventional conditioning base or mixed signals;
- `high`: a strong high-list conditioning/fatty architecture plus additional compatible film/deposition support, without a material unresolved counter-signal;
- conflict fallback: when the detailed formula trace suggests higher deposition but the result is explicitly uncertain, exact-product intended finish materially contradicts it, and no finished-product evidence resolves the conflict, use `moderate` for lean matching and retain the uncertainty.

NEQI Volume Victory keeps `weight_deposition_potential: higher` in the detailed formula trace because `Myristyl Alcohol`, `Cetearyl Alcohol`, and `Cetrimonium Chloride` form a prominent depositing base, while `Amodimethicone`, `Polyquaternium-11`, oat peptide, and emollient support add plausible film routes. This does not prove a heavy finish: several supporting ingredients occur after `Phenoxyethanol`, the under-1% boundary is unknown, all routes interact, and the INCI does not reveal process, molecular weight, emulsion structure, deposited dose, rinse loss, or sensory outcome. Because the exact Volume positioning is a material unresolved counter-signal and `high` would remove fine hair from the broad matching prior, the lean `weight_potential` is `moderate` and uncertain. This is a conservative projection rule, not marketing overriding the formula.

### What can indicate body or volume in a Conditioner formula

No single INCI name proves visible volume. For a rinse-out Conditioner, distinguish three signals:

1. **Body-support candidate:** a dedicated styling or flexible film-forming polymer in a plausible rinse-off delivery context can support temporary fibre stiffness, separation, or apparent diameter. Examples include PVP/VA- or acrylates-family setting polymers and selected styling-oriented polyquaterniums. A substantive protein or peptide film can also be a body candidate, but its presence alone does not establish the deposited amount or visible volume.
2. **Lightness architecture:** a compact conditioning system with low or balanced deposition potential and no strong rich lipid/film stack may preserve existing body. This is not the same as actively creating volume.
3. **Conditioning/deposition architecture:** fatty alcohols, cationic conditioners, amino silicones, oils, and cationic polymers primarily support slip, smoothing, antistatic behaviour, or film deposition. Depending on concentration, molecular weight, processing, and rinse loss, the same architecture may preserve body or weigh it down; ingredient presence alone cannot decide.

Do not use panthenol, botanical extracts, mica/pigments, `silicone-free`, or a `volume` product name as volume proof. Require a finished-product tress method such as projected bundle width/volume, compression, fibre diameter, stiffness, or blinded body assessment before classifying visible volume as tested.

For NEQI, oat peptide and `Polyquaternium-11` are plausible temporary film/body candidates. They are not decisive because the formula also starts with a prominent fatty-alcohol/cationic conditioning base and includes `Amodimethicone`; the INCI does not disclose deposited dose or finished after-feel. `Synthetic Fluorphlogopite`, titanium dioxide, and tin oxide are optical/pigment signals, not volume mechanisms. The current result therefore remains: exact volume positioning is meaningful corroboration, detailed deposition potential stays higher, and the lean weight fallback stays moderate and uncertain until finished-product evidence resolves the conflict.

## How this should be used later

Keep the primary/secondary hierarchy for all future Conditioner research and database-comparison work until an explicit later integration decision replaces it. The Conditioner Personal Plan model already uses independent core axes plus flat `functionalBenefits[]`; future catalog integration may preserve detailed capabilities (`frizz_smoothing`, `shine`, `detangling_slip`, `definition_support`, `color_protection`, and any separately governed repair axis) alongside the research hierarchy. That production possibility does not weaken or remove the current primary/secondary research requirement.
