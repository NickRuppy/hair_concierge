# Personal Plan Bondbuilder evidence

## Scope

External evidence for the Bondbuilder category job, chemical and non-chemical damage context, product differentiation, exact application protocols, safety, and overclaim boundaries. This file does not define Personal Plan thresholds or product ranking. Confirmed product policy lives in `decision.md`; current catalog and runtime behavior are internal evidence and do not belong here.

## Evidence labels

- `strong`: consistent scientific or regulatory support;
- `moderate`: direct evidence with meaningful scope limitations;
- `weak`: indirect, heterogeneous, manufacturer, professional-practice, or community evidence;
- `unknown`: insufficient support for a hard recommendation rule.

## Answer-first findings

### Chemical processing can materially damage the hair fibre

**Evidence: moderate to strong for damage plausibility; not a calibrated person-level score.** Bleaching/lightening, oxidative colouring, perming, and chemical straightening can alter cuticle and cortex structure, reduce tensile properties, and affect disulfide or other structural components. Process chemistry, concentration, pH, time, heat, repetition, overlap, and starting fibre condition materially change the outcome.

Evidence implication: treatment labels can support conservative internal prioritization, especially for lightened and chemically straightened hair. They do not diagnose an individual's damage severity and do not scientifically establish the Personal Plan's exact tier thresholds or proxy weights.

### “Bondbuilder” is not one scientifically uniform mechanism class

**Evidence: moderate for heterogeneity.** The literature and current products use different definitions, mechanisms, test methods, and claims under bond-building or bond-repair language. Peptide-oriented and crosslink/disulfide-oriented descriptions are not validated person-side diagnostic lanes, and marketing terminology alone does not show which product is superior for a given user.

Evidence implication: retain mechanism as a descriptive product fact and provenance field. Do not route users or rank products from `disulfide_crosslink` versus `peptide_chain`, and do not treat one ingredient or marketing phrase as proof of structural efficacy.

### Independent direct comparative evidence is narrow

**Evidence: weak to moderate.** A 2024 hair-tress study compared commercial K18 and OLAPLEX Nº.0 treatments on bleached hair using a standardized laboratory protocol. It reported changes in surface/chemical measurements and some tensile-strength findings, but it did not test untreated or broadly weathered consumers, did not use current OLAPLEX Nº.3PLUS, did not include Epres, and used small ex-vivo samples rather than long-term consumer outcomes.

Evidence implication: the study supports category plausibility for bleached fibre but cannot rank the three current primary catalog products, prove a non-chemical threshold, or validate exact label protocols.

### Non-chemical weathering is real but non-specific

**Evidence: moderate for thermal/mechanical/environmental weathering; weak for Bondbuilder eligibility.** Repeated heat, friction, tension, UV, chlorine, and ordinary weathering can reduce surface quality and fibre strength. Roughness, long/older ends, “high porosity,” poor elasticity, and self-reported breakage can have several causes and do not identify a specific broken-bond mechanism.

Evidence implication: heat reduction/protection, gentler handling, baseline conditioning, and trimming existing split ends remain primary responses. A non-chemical Bondbuilder route is product-policy calibration rather than an evidence-established diagnostic threshold.

### Existing split ends are not restored by a Bondbuilder

**Evidence: strong boundary.** Cosmetic treatments can temporarily smooth or coat damaged ends, but existing split ends are not durably rejoined. Trimming and prevention remain the durable responses.

Evidence implication: split ends may remain contextual but must not qualify a user for Bondbuilder, count as proof of structural repair need, or be described as repaired.

### Product directions differ materially

**Evidence: strong product-specific protocol evidence; not comparative efficacy evidence.** Current official directions differ across application state, order, rinse behavior, Conditioner interaction, waiting time, and course cadence:

- K18 Leave-In Molecular Repair Hair Mask: Shampoo, omit Conditioner, towel-dry, apply, wait four minutes, and leave in; the official course describes four to six consecutive washes followed by use as needed.
- OLAPLEX Nº.3PLUS Complete Repair Treatment: apply to wet hair before Shampoo, wait three minutes, rinse, then Shampoo and condition; current guidance describes use every one to three washes or about weekly depending on need.
- Epres Bond Repair Treatment: dilute and spray onto dry, unwashed hair, leave for at least ten minutes, then wash and care normally; current guidance describes one to two uses weekly followed by maintenance.

Evidence implication: there is no safe universal category timeline. Exact verified product protocol must control executable Stage-3 instructions and course cadence.

### Format can support preference; it does not prove weight or efficacy

**Evidence: strong for observable format/application differences; unknown for a universal weight ranking.** Spray, pre-shampoo cream, and post-wash leave-in formats create real preference and routine tradeoffs. Product form alone does not establish residual heaviness, suitability for fine hair, or superior efficacy because complete formulation, dose, placement, and rinse behavior matter.

Evidence implication: format may differentiate otherwise suitable options when the user expresses a preference. Do not rank Epres, K18, or OLAPLEX by presumed formula weight in V1.

### Finished-product claims require substantiation

**Evidence: strong regulatory principle.** EU cosmetic claims must be supported by adequate and verifiable evidence and must not imply characteristics or functions the product does not have.

Evidence implication: brand claims and mechanism labels establish positioning and protocol provenance, not a universal guarantee of structural reconstruction. Category explanations should remain at strengthening or supporting the resilience of stressed hair.

### Community use is broad but cannot establish efficacy

**Evidence: weak.** Community discussions show use after bleaching/colouring and, secondarily, after frequent heat, long-term weathering, roughness, or breakage. Reports conflict: some users prefer one format or observe improvement, while others report little benefit, dryness, heaviness, or greater benefit from ordinary conditioning.

Evidence implication: community usage supports acknowledging non-chemical interest and format preference, but it cannot define hard need tiers or a product winner.

## Safety and medically adjacent boundary

- Burning, itching, rash, swelling, or another clear reaction supports stopping the suspected product and seeking appropriate assessment when severe or persistent.
- Sudden, unexplained, persistent, or worsening breakage—especially when confused with shedding or hair loss—should not be diagnosed from the quiz or escalated through stronger cosmetic treatment alone.
- Do not infer “protein overload,” a broken-bond diagnosis, cuticle sealing, permanent reconstruction, or internal repair from a home elasticity response, appearance, marketing category, or one ingredient.
- Heat protection and source-control behavior remain primary for heat damage risk even when a Bondbuilder is also used.

## Evidence gaps

- No independent head-to-head evidence compares current Epres, K18, and OLAPLEX Nº.3PLUS under their official protocols.
- No validated consumer diagnostic maps roughness, snapping, breakage, or home elasticity to a specific bond mechanism.
- No strong evidence establishes the Personal Plan's exact chemical or non-chemical tier thresholds.
- No defensible universal Bondbuilder cadence exists independently of the exact product protocol.
- No verified V1 evidence supports a universal product-weight ranking or fine-hair preference among the current primary products.
