# Personal Plan Oil evidence

## Status

External evidence has been reviewed for consumer Oil roles, hair-fibre mechanisms, scalp boundaries, growth/dandruff claims, Heat protection, and current product positioning. Current Hair Concierge behavior remains internal input in `decision.md` and is not efficacy evidence.

## Answer-first findings

### Hair Oil is an umbrella rather than one routine role

Three stable hair-fibre roles recur across product protocols and consumer use:

1. pre-wash treatment through the lengths;
2. small-amount damp leave-on conditioning/preparation;
3. dry finishing through lengths or ends.

One product may support several roles. Pure oil, blend, serum, and dry oil describe formats; Heat protection is a separately verified capability; detangling is a functional outcome; overnight/hot-oil use is a protocol variant.

### Pre-wash benefit cannot be generalized across oils

**Evidence: moderate and narrow.** A direct comparison found that coconut oil reduced protein loss when used before or after washing, while mineral and sunflower oil did not show the same result. The proposed mechanism relates to coconut oil's triglyceride structure and affinity for hair protein.

Product implication: a pre-wash role is legitimate, but “contains oil” or a botanical oil name does not prove the same fibre effect. Require explicit pre-shampoo directions and avoid permanent-repair claims.

### Damp and dry leave-on benefits are cosmetic surface effects

**Evidence: moderate-to-strong mechanism evidence.** Suitable oils, silicones, and film-forming systems can lubricate the fibre, reduce friction and static, improve combability and manageability, smooth the surface, and increase apparent shine. “Sealing moisture” is consumer shorthand rather than evidence that Oil hydrates the hair internally.

Product implication: damp conditioning and dry finishing are supported roles with different application states. Product format, weight, amount, and verified stages determine suitability.

Role ownership implication:

- immediate dry-hair shine and surface polish are the clearest Oil-owned outcomes;
- damp Oil may support softness, flyaways, and slip, but ordinary Leave-in Conditioner has stronger professional support for persistent damp-hair conditioning, detangling, dry/brittle hair, and frizz/flyaways;
- Styling products rather than Oil own durable hold and curl/wave retention;
- pre-wash Oil is a qualified preventive/supportive fibre role rather than a general treatment for existing damage.

### Functional-benefit taxonomy should remain small

The supported V1 benefits are `shine`, `smoothing_frizz_control`, and `slip_manageability`.

- **Shine:** supported as an immediate optical/surface outcome of suitable conditioning films and finishing systems.
- **Smoothing/frizz control:** supported by surface lubrication, reduced friction/static, flyaway control, and product-specific anti-frizz positioning. Humidity resistance requires a finished-product claim rather than inference from “Oil.”
- **Slip/manageability:** supported by lubrication, combability, and reduced friction. Softness is the user-visible expression of the same broad job and does not need a separate catalog axis.

Curl-targeted Oils are marketed, but that does not establish Oil as the source of lasting definition or cast. The most defensible interpretation is that Oil may improve the appearance of curl grouping by smoothing the surface and reducing frizz. Dedicated curl creams and other Styling products explicitly own definition/hold. Using Oil to soften or break an existing cast is therefore an application technique within the dry-finish role, not a fourth functional benefit.

Do not create general Oil benefits for hydration, repair, strengthening, split-end repair, anti-breakage, or color protection. Narrow fibre findings and individual finished-product claims can inform a role or explanation, but they do not establish a reliable category-wide capability. Heat protection remains a separate verified binary product fact.

### Heat protection requires finished-product proof

**Evidence: strong product-claim boundary; moderate mechanism evidence.** Some finished Oil/serum products are explicitly designed and tested as Heat protectants. Oil, silicone, argan, or coconut ingredients alone do not establish protection, temperature limits, or correct activation.

Product implication: require an explicit verified Heat-protection claim and application protocol. Never infer protection from INCI or format.

### Scalp oiling is a real user practice but not a general scalp treatment

Scalp massage/oiling is culturally and commercially common, but it does not establish treatment of scalp disease or hair loss. Ordinary Oil may irritate, occlude, contribute to hairline acne, or aggravate some scalp conditions depending on formulation and user.

Product implication: capture `scalp_use` for inventory understanding but do not recommend or schedule it through the Personal Plan Oil category.

### Scalp cleansing, dandruff treatment, and growth are not ordinary Oil roles

- Pure carrier oil can loosen oil-soluble material but has no surfactant rinse mechanism and normally still requires shampoo; that does not establish scalp cleansing.
- Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis guidance centres on condition-appropriate antifungal/anti-inflammatory care rather than ordinary oiling.
- Evidence for rosemary/castor or generic growth oils is too limited and product-specific to support general recommendations, and Oil use may delay appropriate hair-loss assessment.

Product implication: do not recommend ordinary Oil for cleansing, flakes/itching, seborrheic dermatitis, growth, density, or reduced shedding. A true cleansing oil would be a separate future category.

## Evidence limitations

- Fibre studies do not establish identical benefits across all finished products or oil species.
- Manufacturer positioning establishes intended stage and claims, not independent efficacy.
- Formula, dose, carrier system, fibre condition, contact time, and rinsing materially change results.
- “Dry scalp” is ambiguous and can reflect dryness, irritant/allergic dermatitis, psoriasis, dandruff, or seborrheic dermatitis.
- Essential oils and fragrance introduce irritation and allergic-contact risks.
