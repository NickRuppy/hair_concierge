# Personal Plan Scalp Care evidence

## Scope

This file records the external evidence used to define cosmetic Scalp Care in the Personal Plan. It covers four market-observed roles:

- scalp comfort for dry, tight, sensitive-feeling, or mildly itchy scalp;
- leave-on support marketed for oily or flake-prone scalp;
- cosmetic density, thinning, shedding, growth, or root-support claims;
- scalp exfoliation or scalp-specific residue removal.

It does not define product policy, recommendation thresholds, candidate ranking, or executable protocols. Those decisions live in `decision.md`. Medicines, diagnoses, and treatment plans remain outside the cosmetic category.

## Evidence summary

| Role | What the evidence supports | Strength and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Scalp comfort | A formulated leave-on scalp lotion can improve dryness, scaling, itch, and related comfort measures in selected dry/scaly scalp populations. Gentle moisturising support is plausible when skin is intact. | Low-to-moderate, indirect category evidence. The published result does not establish that every serum, tonic, niacinamide product, ceramide product, or alcohol-rich formula is necessary or effective. |
| Flake/oil adjunct | Dandruff guidance supports appropriate Shampoo as the main cosmetic route. A specific formulated leave-on can show adjunct benefit, but one studied formula cannot validate the whole leave-on market. | Shampoo evidence and professional guidance are stronger. Leave-on evidence is product-specific and does not justify a universal adjunct or a claim that Shampoo contact time is inherently insufficient. |
| Density claim tonic | Cosmetic topical caffeine studies and isolated botanical trials report possible benefit, but studies are heterogeneous and often have important methodological limitations. Label positioning establishes the marketed role, not efficacy. | Limited and mixed. Evidence is insufficient for guaranteed regrowth, stopping hair loss, or a universal product effect. Regulated minoxidil is a separate medicinal route with its own label, indications, and cautions. |
| Scalp exfoliant | Keratolytic or scale-softening approaches can be useful in specific scaly scalp conditions under appropriate guidance. Current Drogerie acid products provide exact pre-wash directions. | Low evidence for broad cosmetic buildup or oily scalp. No source supports one universal exfoliation method or cadence, and condition-specific scale guidance cannot be generalized into a routine cosmetic peeling rule. |

No cosmetic role has sufficiently broad, incremental evidence over appropriate Shampoo/basic care to be treated as universally essential.

## Market and terminology evidence

German mass-market products use overlapping names such as `Kopfhautserum`, `Tonikum`, `Haarwasser`, `Treatment`, `Lotion`, `Fluid`, and `Peeling`. The name does not reliably identify the product's job or rinse behavior.

The reviewed Drogerie sample contains all four roles and both leave-on and rinse-off products:

| Product | Marketed purpose observed | Label-direction pattern observed |
|---|---|---|
| Balea Professional Kopfhautpflege Serum Sensitive | sensitive/comfort support | evening application to dry scalp, massage, wash the next morning; repeat cadence not stated on the reviewed page |
| Eucerin DermoCapillaire 5% Urea Intensiv-Tonikum | dry/itchy scalp comfort | daily on dry or damp scalp; up to twice daily as needed |
| GLISS Scalp Balance Klärendes Serum | oily-scalp/clarifying support | daily on wet or dry scalp; leave on |
| Head & Shoulders DermaXPro Kopfhaut-Feuchtigkeitspflege | scalp moisture and reduced-flake positioning | leave on; reviewed retailer page does not contain a complete cadence |
| L'Oréal Elvital Fiber Booster Anti-Haarverlust Serum | anti-hair-loss/density claim | direct scalp application five to seven times weekly; leave on |
| The Ordinary Multi-Peptide Serum for Hair Density | density claim | daily on clean, dry scalp, preferably at bedtime; leave on |
| Balea Professional 4% AHA Kopfhautpflege Peeling | acid scalp exfoliation | massage into scalp, leave 15–20 minutes, then wash with Shampoo; repeat interval not stated |
| ISANA Professional 2% PHA + 2% AHA Kopfhautpeeling | acid scalp exfoliation | massage into scalp, leave 15–20 minutes, then wash with Shampoo; repeat interval not stated |

These directions demonstrate why optional category inclusion and executable cadence are separate questions. Daily and near-daily leave-ons cannot be reduced to a Mask-like “whenever desired” rule; conversely, a product page that omits repeat cadence cannot borrow a schedule from another product.

Retailer/manufacturer positioning and directions establish market identity and labelled use only. They do not establish clinical efficacy.

## Role-specific evidence

### Scalp comfort

The dry/scaly scalp lotion study supports the possibility that one formulated leave-on can improve dryness, scaling, itch, and quality-of-life measures. It is relevant to the role but does not prove a universal ingredient or product-family effect.

The evidence does not justify the following shortcuts:

- diagnosing a disrupted barrier from dry flakes or itch;
- requiring niacinamide or ceramides for every dry scalp;
- assuming high alcohol content is beneficial;
- assuming any leave-on has longer or more effective active exposure than a suitable Shampoo;
- using a comfort product over burning, painful, weeping, pustular, or actively inflamed skin.

### Flake/oil adjunct

AAD dandruff guidance supports Shampoo-based management and escalation when ordinary use is insufficient or symptoms are concerning. A formulated leave-on antidandruff study provides product-specific adjunct evidence, not evidence for all tonics or serums marketed around flakes.

The available evidence does not support:

- a leave-on for every oily scalp;
- treating a cosmetic serum as a replacement for appropriate dandruff Shampoo;
- inferring yeast, psoriasis, eczema, or contact dermatitis from quiz symptoms;
- stronger cosmetic layering when the scalp is actively irritated.

### Density claims and hair loss

The 2025 topical-caffeine systematic review reports possible positive findings but also heterogeneous products, outcomes, and generally limited study quality. A rosemary-oil comparative trial is insufficient to generalize all rosemary products, concentrations, vehicles, or hair-loss presentations. Peptide and botanical positioning likewise does not establish a finished-product effect.

Regulated minoxidil labels demonstrate a different class of product: a medicine with explicit indications, dosage, cautions, response timing, and stop/escalation conditions. Cosmetic density products must not borrow that evidence or protocol.

The evidence supports uncertainty-aware wording only. It does not support:

- “stops hair loss”;
- “makes hair regrow”;
- “works in every case”;
- ingredient presence as proof of efficacy;
- treating stable low density as active thinning;
- delaying assessment for sudden, patchy, unexplained, painful, burning-associated, or inflamed hair loss.

### Scalp exfoliation

Professional guidance on scalp scale softening is condition-specific and medically adjacent. It supports the existence of keratolytic approaches but cannot be converted into a universal cosmetic buildup rule. Current Drogerie products establish timed pre-Shampoo use, not a common repeat interval.

The evidence does not support:

- oiliness or hormonal change as sufficient proof of exfoliation need;
- acids as universally gentler than physical exfoliation for sensitive skin;
- a universal once-every-two-to-three-weeks cadence;
- DIY salt, sugar, or acid scalp scrubs;
- co-scheduling exfoliation with other intensive scalp or clarifying steps without exact compatibility evidence.

## Safety and escalation boundary

External professional guidance consistently supports conservative handling of active irritation and medically adjacent symptoms:

- burning, pain, pronounced redness/rash, swelling, open or weeping areas, pustules, crusting, or a known product reaction should stop cosmetic optimization;
- persistent or recurrent dry, scaly, itchy, or red scalp may have overlapping causes that the quiz cannot diagnose;
- sudden, patchy, unexplained, or symptom-associated hair loss warrants assessment rather than a stronger cosmetic recommendation;
- product reaction guidance takes precedence over role fit and cadence.

Mild intermittent sensitivity or itch on intact scalp is not equivalent to these stop signals, but the current quiz value `irritated` combines both states and therefore requires clarification before deterministic cosmetic routing.

## Conflicts and rejected overclaims

The influencer/expert notes are useful market and usage context. The following claims were not strong enough to become hard Personal Plan rules:

| Claim considered | Evidence conclusion |
|---|---|
| Scalp problems generally require Serum/Scrub because Shampoo cannot deliver enough active ingredient | Rejected as a universal claim. Product-, condition-, and regimen-specific evidence is required. |
| Rapidly oily scalp requires pre-cleansing or exfoliation | Rejected. Oiliness alone is not proof of scalp buildup or exfoliation need. |
| Acid exfoliation is always gentler than a physical Scrub | Rejected. Tolerance depends on formulation, use, skin state, and method. |
| Shampoo plus salt/sugar is a safe DIY Scrub | Rejected from product guidance. |
| Niacinamide and ceramides make a therapeutic serum essential for dehydrated scalp | Rejected as a universal requirement. Ingredient plausibility is not finished-product evidence. |
| Growth serums should show results after four to eight weeks | Rejected as a general cosmetic expectation. Exact products and study endpoints vary. |
| Every scalp peeling should be used once every two to three weeks | Rejected. The reviewed exact directions do not establish that interval. |

## Evidence limitations

- The four roles are market-derived product purposes, not diagnoses.
- Many sources evaluate one finished product or one condition-specific intervention; they do not validate the whole role.
- Retailer pages may omit package directions and can change. Exact identity and instructions require normal Product Intake verification before launch.
- The current quiz does not capture duration, onset, pattern, prior appropriate Shampoo use, treatment adherence, response, diagnosis, or medication context in enough detail to infer treatment failure.
- Evidence strength can change with future finished-product trials. It should affect confidence and wording, not silently reclassify owned products.
