# Personal Plan Mask evidence

## Status

This file preserves the evidence-sensitive conclusions reviewed during the Mask category pass. The source register separates scientific, regulatory, and manufacturer evidence and states the relevant limitations. Current recommendation-engine behavior belongs in `decision.md` as internal input and is not efficacy evidence.

## Answer-first findings

### Ordinary masks provide periodic intensive conditioning, not permanent repair

Rinse-out Masks primarily improve surface conditioning, lubrication, slip, softness, static/friction reduction, shine, and manageability. These effects can be valuable for dry, rough, tangled, chemically treated, or mechanically stressed lengths, but they do not permanently reconstruct damaged fibre or reverse split ends.

Product implication: keep Conditioner as baseline care, Leave-in as persistent post-wash care, and Mask as periodic intensity. Avoid “nutrient replacement” and permanent-repair explanations.

### Product families marketed as masks do not share one protocol

Moisture-led, smoothing, repair-support, and protein-oriented products overlap substantially. Color-depositing masks, scalp masks, and protocol-specific Bondbuilders have materially different jobs or safety/application rules.

Product implication: ordinary Mask is one category with independent weight, care-direction, repair-support, functional, and protocol facts. Exclude color, scalp, and specialized bond-treatment products from its V1 logic.

### Market purposes collapse into three overlapping commercial clusters

**Evidence: strong for current market positioning; not efficacy evidence.** Representative mass-market, premium, curly-hair, and professional portfolios repeatedly use three broad positions:

1. conditioning/nourishing for intensive softness, slip, and manageability;
2. repair/strengthening support for damaged, brittle, or chemically treated hair;
3. smoothing/frizz control for surface smoothness, tameability, and shine.

Individual products regularly combine these claims. Garnier markets repair, nourishment, plumping/fine-hair, and curl variants within the same Hair Food family, while the Kérastase Curl Manifesto Mask combines nutrition, smoothness, frizz reduction, shine, and stronger-hair claims. This overlap argues against mutually exclusive Mask types or a forced product-level primary benefit.

Product implication: intensive conditioning is implicit in the category, repair intensity remains the independent `repairSupportLevel`, and the remaining specific outcomes may be stored as flat benefits. Texture and color positioning belong primarily to suitability metadata rather than new Mask purposes.

### Marketing synonyms should not become separate catalog values

**Evidence: strong market-language finding.** Moisture, hydration, nourishment, softness, and dry-hair care generally describe overlapping intensive-conditioning positions. Anti-frizz, smoothing, silky, taming, and shine also overlap substantially as surface-conditioning outcomes. “Protein Mask” describes an ingredient/care direction rather than proof of a diagnosed protein deficiency.

Product implication: merge moisture/nourishment language into `careDirection` plus the ordinary Mask baseline; use `smoothing_frizz_control`, `detangling_slip`, and `shine` only as non-exclusive flat benefits. Do not create distinct curl, color, protein, hydration, or nourishment Mask types.

### Common ingredient systems support cosmetic surface effects, not permanent reconstruction

**Evidence: moderate-to-strong mechanism evidence.** Ordinary Masks commonly combine cationic surfactants or amidoamines, fatty alcohols, silicones or other film formers, emollients, oils/butters, humectants, and cationic polymers. These systems can reduce charge, static, friction, and combing force and improve slip, softness, manageability, and apparent shine.

Hydrolyzed proteins, amino acids, and film-forming polymers may deposit on damaged fibre and provide temporary strength or feel support. Acidic systems may reduce some consequences of alkaline swelling. The result depends on the complete formulation, molecular size, charge, concentration, vehicle, contact time, fibre condition, and rinsing—not the presence of one recognizable ingredient.

Product implication: use complete verified product evidence to tag weight, care direction, repair support, and benefits. Never infer richness from one oil/butter or infer repair performance from one protein/amino-acid ingredient.

### There is no evidence-based universal every-N-washes cadence

Professional guidance and manufacturer protocols commonly position Masks as periodic or weekly intensive care, but exact frequencies vary by formula, hair response, and routine. No universal clinical interval supports prescribing every third wash to every eligible user.

Product implication: category policy may calibrate a conservative deterministic cadence, but must describe it as a recommendation rather than a scientific constant. Optional use remains genuinely on demand.

### Situational need is real but usually not observable from onboarding

The clearest specific situational guidance exists around exposures such as chlorinated swimming. Heavy Styling, clarifying, or heat exposure does not by itself prove that every person needs a Mask afterwards; conditioning, exposure prevention, and the actual state of the lengths matter.

Product implication: keep an optional intensive-care day available without asking users to predict rare events. Promote Mask to a scheduled Basis only from the confirmed profile-level inclusion rules.

### Conditioner relationship is product-specific within a wash

Masks are additional periodic care at portfolio level, but exact usage instructions differ: some replace Conditioner for that wash, while other protocols place Conditioner before or after the Mask.

Product implication: store a verified Conditioner relationship and compile the exact routine from it. Do not enforce one universal Mask/Conditioner order.

## Evidence limitations and follow-up

- Direct comparative trials for exact consumer Mask cadence are limited.
- Creator material may inform application technique but cannot establish safety or efficacy rules by itself.
- Exact timing, amount, weekly cap, application area, and Conditioner relationship require verified product-specific directions when available.
- Ingredient lists and formulas vary by country and change over time; preserve market, source URL, and review date.
- Manufacturer pages establish current positioning and protocol, not independent proof of effectiveness.
